**Project:** Aegis-40 — a 40 MWe soluble-boron-free integral pressurised-water reactor
**Team:** NUClearly, New Uzbekistan University · Team ID 995912 · Application ID 5300592

**Finals:** 1–4 September 2026, TENMAK İstanbul Çekmece Campus, Türkiye

## What the design is

Aegis-40 is a small modular reactor producing **125 MW of thermal power and 40 MW of electricity**,
designed for a coastal site at Sinop, Türkiye. Three features distinguish it from operating and
announced designs:

- **No soluble boron.** Reactivity is controlled entirely by solid burnable absorbers and control rods,
  which removes an entire class of dilution accidents from the plant.
- **No reactor coolant pumps.** Primary flow is driven by natural circulation, so loss of electrical
  power is not a loss-of-flow event.
- **Three products.** Electricity, district heat and hydrogen, using heat that would otherwise be
  rejected to the sea.

The reactor operates for **six years without refuelling**, and can cool itself for more than **ten days**
with no electrical power, no seawater and no operator action.

## Scope of the work completed

| Domain | Method |
|---|---|
| Neutronics and fuel depletion | OpenMC 0.15.3 Monte Carlo, ENDF/B-VIII.0 nuclear data, full three-dimensional core model |
| Thermal-hydraulics | OpenFOAM conjugate heat-transfer model with three independent critical-heat-flux correlations |
| Secondary cycle and cogeneration | DWSIM process model on IAPWS-IF97 properties |
| Radiation shielding | Monte Carlo transport, 60-year vessel fluence and occupational dose |
| Safety analysis | Deterministic design-basis event matrix and probabilistic core-damage frequency |
| Economics | Levelised cost of electricity with sensitivity analysis |

All results are validated against international benchmark experiments (ICSBEP criticality benchmarks
and the BEAVRS operating-reactor benchmark) and delivered with a **digital appendix** containing input
files, run settings and outputs so that every number in the report is reproducible.

# Notes for Samira — read before sending

**1 · Check the advisor's name spelling and role.** You wrote *"Alisher Santullaev"*; slide 5 of the
deck lists *"Alisher Sanetullaev"* as a **team member**. Whichever is correct, the letter and the deck
must agree — and if he is the advisor rather than a competing team member, **slide 5 should say so**,
because a jury reading "six-member undergraduate team" while a staff member is listed among them is an
avoidable question.

**2 · Route it correctly.** In most universities a request of this kind goes to the Rector **through**
the department head or dean, not directly from a student. I have drafted it from you with endorsement
lines for the advisor and the head of department, which is the safest form. Ask the department office
what their preferred route and reference format is — a letter in the wrong format gets returned rather
than refused.

**3 · Get the quotations first.** A request with a specific, itemised, quoted total is approved far more
often than one asking for "travel costs". Attach real flight and hotel prices as Annex A.

**4 · Send it now, not next week.** Procurement and finance approval takes time, and prices to İstanbul
rise as the date approaches. If a decision is needed before a fixed date, say so explicitly in the
covering email.

**5 · Language.** If your administration works in Uzbek or Russian, tell me and I will produce a
translated version. Formal register in Uzbek administrative correspondence differs from a direct
translation, so it is worth doing properly rather than machine-translating.

**6 · If the budget is tight**, consider asking for a defined subset — for example air travel only, with
the advisor covering accommodation, or shared accommodation with the team. A partial request that is
approved is worth more than a full request that is declined.
