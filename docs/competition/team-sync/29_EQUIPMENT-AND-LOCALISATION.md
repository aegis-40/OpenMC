# Equipment list and localisation — who makes what, and how many

**Written after the finals**, in response to the jury asking where the turbines, pumps and other
equipment would be ordered from, and how many are needed.

---

# PART 1 — The honest starting point

**You do not have a full bill of materials, and the FER says so.** §8.12 refers to completing a
bottom-up cost account *"once the §8.10 equipment list is fixed"* — so a component-by-component BOM is
named as remaining work, not something we omitted by accident.

**What the FER does commit to** is the major-component set, the building list with footprints, the
critical piping, and a **staged localisation strategy**. That is the right level for a detailed design
study, and it is what to answer from.

> **Opening line:** *"We have the major-component definition and a staged localisation pathway. A
> component-level bill of materials is named in the report as remaining work — it belongs with the
> bottom-up cost estimate, and neither exists yet."*

# PART 3 — What the design removes from the supply chain ⭐

**Lead with this. It is the strongest localisation argument you have**, because it is about
components you *do not need to buy at all*:

| Not required | Why |
|---|---|
| **Reactor coolant pumps** | natural circulation — and RCPs are among the hardest, most export-controlled nuclear components in existence |
| **RCP seals, seal-injection and seal-cooling systems** | no pumps |
| **Boron make-up, recovery and purification trains** | soluble-boron-free |
| **External pressuriser, surge line and spray system** | integral pressuriser |
| **Large-bore primary piping and its supports** | integral vessel |
| **External CRDM pressure housings** | in-vessel drives |
| **Safety-class rotating machinery, essentially all of it** | every credited system is passive or gravity-driven |

> **"The cheapest component to localise is the one you do not have to buy. We removed the reactor
> coolant pumps, the boron plant, the surge line and the external rod-drive housings — which are among
> the hardest items in a conventional PWR supply chain."**

# PART 4 — Localisation, in three tiers

The FER's own position:

> *"The localization claim is therefore **limited to a credible pathway**. Early domestic participation
> is most suitable for civil structures, modular buildings, non-safety piping, HVAC, fire protection,
> conventional electrical systems, radiation-monitoring equipment, digital dashboards, TCES modules and
> hydrogen balance-of-plant equipment. Nuclear fuel, cladding, reactor internals, pressure-boundary
> components and qualified valves stay long-qualification items."*

## Tier 1 — domestic from day one

Civil works and concrete · modular steel buildings · non-safety piping and HVAC · fire protection ·
conventional electrical distribution · **154 kV transformers** · **standby diesel generators** ·
circulating-water and BOP pumps · radiation monitoring · seawater intake and outfall civil works ·
digital dashboards and the twin · engineering, layout and analysis.

**Concrete Turkish strengths worth naming:**
- **Transformers** — Türkiye has a substantial transformer manufacturing and export industry.
- **Diesel gensets** — MW-class standby sets are manufactured domestically.
- **Industrial pumps** — an established domestic sector for BOP duty.

## Tier 2 — domestic within a decade, with qualification

The **40 MWe saturated-steam turbine-generator** · non-safety heat exchangers · the **TCES sorption
modules** · hydrogen balance-of-plant and compression · non-safety I&C and the distributed control
system · fabrication of internals under licence.

**The turbine is the strongest Tier-2 argument, and it is not obvious — make it explicitly:**

> **A 40 MWe saturated-steam turbine is not a nuclear-specific machine.** It is the same class as a
> geothermal turbine, and Türkiye operates one of the world's larger geothermal fleets on exactly this
> kind of low-pressure, wet-steam machine. **The domestic operating and maintenance experience already
> exists.** Compare that with a 1,100 MWe nuclear turbine, which perhaps five companies worldwide can
> build.

**TCES is the other one.** Zeolite sorption modules are chemical-process equipment, not nuclear
equipment — and Türkiye has substantial natural zeolite resources and industrial-minerals capability.
*(Note: the reference material is synthetic 13X, not natural clinoptilolite — be precise if pushed.)*

## Tier 3 — imported for the foreseeable future

**Fuel** — UO₂ assemblies with Gd/Er. Global fabricators only.
**Enrichment** — a service, not a resource. Türkiye has uranium but no conversion or enrichment.
**The RPV forging** — SA-508 heavy forging.
**Reactor internals and pressure-boundary components.**
**Safety-class qualified valves** — MSIVs, MFIVs, ADS.
**Class 1E I&C platform** — qualified FPGA/PLC to IEC 60880; a very small global vendor set.
**Boron-10 enrichment** — a handful of suppliers worldwide.
**The SOE stack** — solid-oxide electrolysis is a small, specialised market.

# PART 5 — Two arguments that land well

## The vessel is deliberately within a wide supplier base

**3.414 m outside diameter, under the 3.5 m shipping envelope.** That is not only about transport:

> **A 3.4-metre vessel can be forged by many more suppliers than a 5-metre one.** Large-PWR vessels
> need the handful of heavy-forging presses in the world. At our size the supplier pool is
> substantially wider — which matters for price, for schedule, and for not being captive to a single
> vendor.

## Boron-10 — the feedstock is Turkish

Türkiye holds roughly **70 % of world boron reserves** (Eti Maden). Your control rods use B₄C enriched
to 90 % B-10.

> **The raw material is about as domestic as a material can be. Only the isotope-separation step is
> imported — and that is a service, not a resource.**

## And a policy alignment worth quoting

Türkiye's **Hydrogen Technologies Strategy and Roadmap** targets 2 GW of electrolyser capacity by 2030,
5 GW by 2035 and 70 GW by 2053, explicitly emphasising **domestic electrolyser components**. Your
8 MWe SOE module sits inside a declared national industrial programme, not outside it.

# PART 6 — What to say

**"Where would you buy the turbine?"**
> "It is a forty-megawatt saturated-steam machine — not a nuclear-specific item at that size. The same
> class of turbine runs in geothermal plants, and Türkiye has one of the larger geothermal fleets in the
> world, so the domestic operating experience is already there. Several international vendors build this
> class, and it is a realistic medium-term localisation target. A gigawatt-class nuclear turbine would
> not be."

**"And the pumps?"**
> "That is the good news — **we do not have reactor coolant pumps.** Natural circulation removes them,
> and with them the seals, the seal-injection system and their power supplies. The pumps we do have are
> feedwater, condensate and circulating water, all conventional balance-of-plant, and Türkiye has an
> established pump industry. There is essentially no safety-class rotating machinery in this design."

**"What must be imported?"**
> "Fuel, enrichment, the vessel forging, reactor internals, qualified safety valves, the Class 1E
> instrumentation platform, boron-10 enrichment and the electrolyser stack. We do not claim otherwise —
> the report says the localisation claim is limited to a credible pathway."

**"How many components in total?"**
> "We have the major-component definition — one vessel, one integral steam generator, sixteen rod
> assemblies and drives, thirty-seven fuel assemblies, two passive heat-removal trains, one
> turbine-generator, one condenser, two diesel generators, thirteen buildings across about 4,800 square
> metres of footprint. A full component-level bill of materials is named in the report as remaining
> work, and it belongs with the bottom-up cost estimate."

**"Could Türkiye build this?"**
> "Not alone, and not at the first unit. What Türkiye could do from day one is the civil works, the
> buildings, the conventional electrical plant, transformers, diesel generators, balance-of-plant pumps
> and piping, radiation monitoring and all the engineering. Within a decade the turbine, the heat
> exchangers, the thermal store and hydrogen equipment. What stays imported is fuel, the pressure
> boundary and safety-class instrumentation — which is the same position every newcomer nuclear country
> starts from, including Türkiye at Akkuyu."
