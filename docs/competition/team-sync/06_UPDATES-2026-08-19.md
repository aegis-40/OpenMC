## 2. MSLB re-analysed on the real core — Azamkhon & Adilbek, this changes your answer

### The problem

Our shipped MSLB cases (N12 / N12B) were computed on the **superseded rod basis**: 12 CRAs with natural-boron B₄C, bank ≈ 13,409 pcm, k_ARI hot = 1.0026. The design we present is **N5C**: 16 CRAs at 90 % enriched B-10, bank **21,509 pcm**, k_ARI hot = 0.9273.

The MSLB analysis was missing **~7,500 pcm of shutdown depth**. So the numbers on the deck described a core we'd abandoned.

Separately, the wording was a liability. The slide said EBIS *"caps the return-to-power that rods alone cannot."* The SBF literature says the opposite — that high rod worth is exactly what makes MSLB manageable in boron-free cores (KIT TRACE/PARCS on a boron-free SMART core, *NED* 2019). A judge who knows that literature had an easy opening.

### What we did

Re-ran the full cooldown on the N5C basis: `openmc_model/scripts/run_mslb_n5c.py`, OpenMC 0.15.3 / ENDF/B-VIII.0, medium statistics (matching the originals), 14 eigenvalue runs, ~22 min.

**Validation:** the cold stuck-rod point came out at **1.03115** against N5C's independently stored `k_stuck_cold = 1.0311`, and the script independently selected the same most-reactive rod (3,2). Clean like-for-like replacement.

### The result — much better than what we were claiming

| T (K) | superseded (12 CRA, nat. B₄C) | **final (16 CRA, 90 % B-10)** | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 556 | 1.0183 | **0.9345** | subcritical |
| 523 | 1.0377 | **0.9565** | subcritical |
| 473 | 1.0611 | **0.9787** | subcritical |
| 423 | 1.0867 | 1.0039 | supercritical |
| 294 | **1.1142** | **1.0311** | supercritical |

The old case was supercritical **from the very first point** — the rods couldn't hold the core even at hot conditions. On the real core, **rods alone hold it subcritical from full power down to ~443 K (~170 °C)**.

**And the residual boron requirement:**

```
    0 ppm  k_adj 1.0374  fail
  500 ppm  k_adj 0.9781  fail
 1000 ppm  k_adj 0.9288  PASS     →  785 ppm required for k_adj ≤ 0.95
 2000 ppm  k_adj 0.8510  PASS
```

**EBIS needs ~785 ppm, not the 3,000 ppm we credit** (which gives k_adj = 0.790). That is a **3.8× margin** we weren't claiming.

### The answer to have ready

> "Rod worth handles the hot trip **and most of the cooldown** — at 21,509 pcm the core stays subcritical from full power down to about 170 °C with the most-reactive rod stuck. EBIS covers only the deep cold state, where a boron-free core is inherently supercritical. And we need about 785 ppm to do it; we credit 3,000."

This is now *consistent* with the SBF literature instead of contradicting it.

## 4. Publication strategy — for tomorrow

Full ranked analysis in **`05_PUBLICATION-STRATEGY.md`** (also at https://claude.ai/code/artifact/51b82092-2ec3-4009-919c-dc035f07c592).

Short version:
- **Recommended paper: in-vessel CRDM dissolves the rod-ejection constraint on SBF core design.** SBF cores need high total rod worth → forces high individual rod worth → severe rod-ejection accident, and people actively design around this. Integral PWRs with in-vessel CRDM eliminate rod ejection by construction. **Nobody has connected the two literatures.**
- **Today's MSLB run is already the core dataset for it.** `k_stuck_cold = 1.031` at 21,509 pcm and the 785 ppm residual requirement *are* the quantified "how much boron dependency survives the rod-worth upgrade" answer.
- **Do not write the peaking/margin paper** — MIT published it (*Nuclear Technology*, 2024).
- **Free routes exist:** Elsevier/T&F subscription track costs nothing; NET has no APC to 8 pages.
