# PART 1 — THE TURBINE, ANSWERED PROPERLY ⭐

This is the question that was actually asked, and Türkiye's own industry answers it.

**Aegis-40's turbine is a 40 MWe saturated-steam machine** — main steam at 4.5 MPa / 296 °C, 57.75 kg/s,
tandem HP/IP/LP, 42.79 MW shaft. **That is the geothermal turbine class, not the nuclear turbine class.**

## Türkiye already buys exactly this machine

| Plant | Machine | Supplier |
|---|---|---|
| Kızıldere II | 60 MW geothermal steam turbine | **Fuji Electric** |
| Kızıldere III | flash steam turbine system | **Toshiba** |
| Germencik | steam turbine (two units) | **Mitsubishi Heavy Industries** |

**Three international OEMs have each delivered a wet-steam turbine of our size class to a Turkish site.**
This is not a hypothetical procurement route — it is an existing, repeated one.

## And the domestic base is already substantial

Türkiye has reached roughly **55 % domestic content in geothermal power equipment**. Domestically
produced today: **generators, power electronics, turbines, exhaust and lubrication systems, speed
control, steam injectors.** The segment supports about **50 component manufacturers and 350
sub-suppliers, employing ~50,000 people.**

> **The answer to "where do you order the turbine" is: the same place Türkiye already orders its
> geothermal turbines — and into a domestic supply base that is already 55 % localised for this exact
> class of machine.**

**Say this precisely:** naming Fuji, Toshiba and MHI is evidence that the machine class is routinely
delivered to Türkiye. It is not a claim that we have a commercial relationship with any of them.

# PART 3 — HOW THE CONSTRUCTION IS MANAGED

## The delivery model — and why not Akkuyu's

**Akkuyu is Build-Own-Operate: the vendor owns the plant.** That is exactly why Türkiye can reach 56 %
local content and still hold no design ownership. Türkiye has already signalled the shift — Sinop and
Thrace are being pursued as **joint ventures with mandatory localisation**.

**Proposed structure for Aegis-40:**

| Role | Who |
|---|---|
| Owner / operator | Turkish SPV under the state nuclear entity |
| Architect-Engineer | Domestic AE paired with an experienced international nuclear AE |
| **Nuclear island** | Foreign supplier **under technology transfer**, units 1–4 |
| **Conventional island + BOP** | **Domestic EPC** |
| **Civil works** | **Domestic** |
| Commissioning | Joint, transitioning to domestic by unit 3 |
| Regulator | NDK, engaged from Year 1 |

## Türkiye does not need to learn how to build things

> **48 Turkish contractors are in ENR's 2026 Top 250 International Contractors — second in the world
> after China (75).** Seven are in the global top 100, two in the top 50, with $24.9 bn of international
> work in the last year.

Construction management capability is not the gap. **The gap is nuclear-grade QA on a specific scope,
and Akkuyu has already been the training ground for 400+ firms.**

## The design does two things that protect the schedule ⭐

**1 — The integral primary system removes site pipe welding.** There is no external primary loop, no
surge line, no large-bore primary piping and no RCP connections. **Large-bore safety-class field welding
is historically where nuclear projects lose schedule** — inspection, rework, NDE backlogs. Aegis-40 moves
that work into the vessel shop.

**2 — Small footprint, modular buildings.** 13 structures over ~4,840 m² of building footprint, site
envelope ~31,600 m² governed by the 123 m hydrogen stand-off (RG 1.91), not by the buildings. Modular
steel construction is a domestic strength.

## Construction sequence

```
1. Site prep, excavation, seawater intake/outfall civil        ── domestic
2. Basemat and nuclear-island civil                            ── domestic
3. Open-top module installation, heavy lift                    ── domestic EPC, modules in factory
   ↳ modules fabricated in parallel with civil work, not after it
4. RPV set (single lift, ~270 t)                               ── partner-supervised
5. Mechanical + electrical completion, BOP                     ── domestic
6. Cold hydro → hot functional testing                         ── joint
7. Fuel load → startup physics testing → power ascension       ── joint, NDK oversight
```

**The scheduling principle throughout: parallel, not serial.** Licensing runs alongside procurement;
module fabrication runs alongside civil works. That is the entire basis of the five-year claim.

# PART 4 — FIVE-YEAR SCHEDULE

| Year | Licensing | Procurement | Construction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Y1** | NDK pre-licensing entry; TENMAK call | Nuclear-island partner selected | Site characterisation |
| **Y2** | **Site licence** | TSE qualification of domestic vendors; **long-lead RPV forging + fuel contract** | Site prep, excavation |
| **Y3** | **Construction licence** | Turbine, condenser, transformers ordered | **First concrete**; basemat |
| **Y4** | Inspection programme | Modules in factory | Civil complete; module installation |
| **Y5** | Operating-licence application | — | **RPV set**; mechanical completion; commissioning start |

**Critical path: the RPV forging (~2–3 years) and the construction licence.** Both must start in Y1–Y2 or
the schedule does not hold. Say this openly — naming your own critical path is what makes a schedule
credible.

# PART 5 — SPOKEN ANSWERS

**"Where do you order the turbine?"**
> "From the same place Türkiye already orders geothermal turbines. Ours is a forty-megawatt
> saturated-steam machine — the same class, not a nuclear-specific item. Fuji Electric supplied a sixty
> megawatt unit to Kızıldere II, Toshiba supplied Kızıldere III, Mitsubishi supplied Germencik. All three
> have delivered this class of machine to Turkish sites. And Türkiye is already at about fifty-five
> percent domestic content in geothermal power equipment, with roughly fifty component makers and three
> hundred and fifty sub-suppliers, so there is a real base to localise into."

**"How would you actually manage the construction?"**
> "Domestic EPC for the conventional island, balance of plant and all civil works; the nuclear island
> from a foreign supplier under technology transfer for the first units; a domestic architect-engineer
> paired with an experienced international one. Not the build-own-operate model used at Akkuyu — that
> model is precisely why Türkiye has fifty-six percent local content and no design ownership.
>
> And construction capability is genuinely not the constraint here: forty-eight Turkish contractors are
> in ENR's top two hundred and fifty international contractors list, which puts Türkiye second in the
> world after China."

**"Why should we believe a five-year schedule?"**
> "Because two things are parallel that are usually serial. Licensing runs alongside procurement rather
> than after it, and module fabrication runs alongside civil works. And the design helps: this is an
> integral reactor, so there is no external primary loop, no surge line and no large-bore primary piping
> to weld in the field. Large-bore safety-class field welding is where nuclear projects usually lose
> their schedule, and we have moved that work into the vessel shop. The critical path is the vessel
> forging, at two to three years, and the construction licence — if either slips, the schedule slips, and
> I would rather say that than pretend otherwise."

**"What can you order today, and what can't you?"**
> "Today, domestically: all civil works, the buildings, non-safety piping and HVAC, the tanks, the
> containment structure, transformers and switchyard, diesel generators, balance-of-plant pumps,
> radiation monitoring, the thermal store, and all the software. Imported: the fuel, the vessel forging,
> the internals, safety-class qualified valves, the Class 1E instrumentation platform, boron-10
> enrichment, and the electrolyser stack. The boron ore itself is Turkish — Eti Maden holds around
> seventy percent of world reserves — so only the isotope separation step is bought in, and that is a
> service rather than a resource."
