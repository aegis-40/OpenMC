# Realization Strategy — the slide, rewritten for an industrialist

**Second version.** The first draft was a procurement-and-licensing slide: get a licence, use existing
suppliers, apply to a programme. Any of the twenty teams in that room could have shown it. It told
Turkish executives facts about Türkiye they know better than we do, and the three "points" were a
description of the standard process, not a strategy.

**This version argues from manufacturing**, because that is the only axis on which a student design
beats a bought APR1400 — and because our own FER already says so.

---

# THE SENTENCE THAT SHOULD HAVE BEEN POINT ONE

From our own §8.12.5, about the $8,250/kWe capital number:

> *"the derived value sits above the vendor target, so competitiveness is **explicitly a bet on modular
> production** driving costs below the scaling curve"*
> *"cost competitiveness is a **factory/series-production objective**, carried as the upper band"*

Read that plainly: **$8,250/kWe is our nth-of-a-kind cost, and it is still above the vendor target.**
There is no version of Aegis-40 that is economic as a one-off. We wrote that down ourselves.

**So the industrial strategy is not a companion to the business case. It is the business case.** The
question "where do you buy the turbine" is the wrong question, and the honest answer is: *you do not buy
equipment for one reactor — you build a line, or you do not build this at all.*

Lead with that. It is the only opening that earns a manufacturer's attention, and it is unarguable
because it is a quotation from our own report.

# THE SLIDE

**Title:** `Realization Strategy — A Production Line, Not a Project`
**Subtitle:** `Our own economics only close at unit N. That decides the strategy.`

## 1 — 56 % LOCAL CONTENT IS A SUPPLY NUMBER, NOT A TECHNOLOGY NUMBER

**Türkiye manufactures over half of Akkuyu by value and owns none of the design.**

- 400+ qualified Turkish firms, ~$7.5 bn of supply value, TSE the material approval body since 2020 —
  a real achievement, and **the wrong axis to measure sovereignty on**
- Rebar, cable tray, valves and tanks are localised. The reactor is not.
- **You cannot export a reactor you bought.** Türkiye has stated the ambition to be a regional nuclear
  exporter — 40 MWe cogeneration is the right product for markets that cannot absorb 1,400 MWe, and an
  owned design is the only kind that is sellable
- *Supplying a platform and owning one are different industries*

## 2 — 5 GW ÷ 40 MWe = 125 UNITS. THAT IS A FACTORY.

**Türkiye's declared SMR target is 5 GW. At our rating that is 125 units — about $330 M each.**

- We do not need to win all of it. **10 % is a 12-unit, ~$4 bn order book** — already past the threshold
  that justifies fixed tooling
- Our $8,250/kWe **already assumes** the ×0.55 modularity and ×0.72 learning credits. Those are not
  discounts you receive for building one reactor; they are earned on a line
- The deliverable at Year 5 is therefore **not one reactor — it is a rate.** Two units per year
- *This is the number we should have put on a slide from the beginning*

## 3 — THE QUALIFICATION PROBLEM IS TWO ITEMS, NOT TWENTY

**Because there is no safety-class rotating machinery, everything hard collapses onto two targets.**

- Designed out: **reactor coolant pumps, seals and seal injection, the boron plant, the surge line,
  external CRDM housings.** The classic nuclear qualification nightmare is mostly absent
- **① Pressure boundary** — 3.414 m OD sits inside a far wider fabrication base than a 4.5–5 m large-PWR
  vessel. Licence-build with the partner for units 1–4, qualify domestically by unit ~5
- **② Class 1E I&C** — buy the qualified platform for units 1–3, run a parallel domestic FPGA
  qualification to IEC 60880 on a five-year clock
- Everything else is either already localised at Akkuyu or is not nuclear equipment at all
- *A two-target programme is winnable in five years. A twenty-target one is not*

## Bottom bar — a manufacturing ramp, not a licensing ramp

| Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NDK pre-licensing entry · pressure-boundary partner selected · tooling concept | Site licence · TSE Tier-1 vendor qualification · **long-lead forging order** | Construction licence · **module factory operational** | **First concrete** · units 2–3 already in fabrication | Unit 1 vessel set · **line at ~2 units/yr** |

**The Year-3 milestone is the factory, not the licence.** Target ≥ 70 % local content at unit 1, rising
with the line *(our target, not a published plan)*

> modularity and learning credits applied. Our report says plainly that it sits above the vendor target,

> design that is economic as a single unit. That is not a weakness we are hiding; it is the fact that
> determines the whole strategy. **You do not buy equipment for one reactor. You build a line, or you do
> not build this.**
>
> That gives us three points.
>
> **First — fifty-six percent local content at Akkuyu is a supply number, not a technology number.**
> Türkiye manufactures more than half of that plant by value and owns none of the design. Four hundred
> qualified firms is a real achievement and we would inherit every one of them. But rebar and cable tray
> are localised; the reactor is not. And you cannot export a reactor you bought — which matters, because
> Türkiye has said it wants to be a regional exporter, and forty megawatts of cogeneration is the right
> product for markets that cannot absorb fourteen hundred.
>
> **Second — the declared national target is five gigawatts of SMRs. At forty megawatts, that is a
> hundred and twenty-five units, about three hundred and thirty million dollars each.** We do not need to
> win all of it. Ten percent is twelve units and four billion dollars, which is already enough to justify
> fixed tooling. So what we are proposing to have at year five is not one reactor. It is a rate — about
> two units a year.
>
> **Third — and this is what makes five years arguable at all — the qualification problem is two items,
> not twenty.** We have no reactor coolant pumps, no seals, no boron plant, no surge line. There is

> units, qualify domestically by unit five. Buy the I&C platform for units one to three and run a
> domestic qualification in parallel. Two targets, five years. That is a programme you can actually
> staff.
>
> **The year-three milestone on our timeline is the factory, not the licence.** That is the difference
> between this and a construction project."

# WHAT A MANUFACTURER WILL ASK THAT A REGULATOR WILL NOT

Be ready for these. Getting caught without them is what makes a strategy slide look like decoration.

**"What is your takt time and what is the bottleneck?"**
> "At two units a year the binding constraint is the pressure vessel — roughly two to three years of
> forging and fabrication lead, so you need two vessels a year in the pipe from year two onward. That is
> precisely why the pressure boundary is target number one and why it cannot stay a single-source import.
> Fuel is the second constraint and it is contractual rather than industrial. Everything else — the
> internals, the modules, the balance of plant — is inside a two-year cycle and is not what sets the
> rate."

**"What does the tooling cost, and who pays for it?"**
> "We have not costed the factory, and I will not invent a number. What our economics do contain is the
> ×0.55 modularity and ×0.72 learning credits, which is the same statement in a different form: those
> credits are the return on that tooling investment. Sizing it properly is the next piece of work, and it
> belongs with the bottom-up cost account our report already names as outstanding."

**"Why would we not simply license an existing SMR design?"**
> "You might, and for the first units that could well be the faster route. The argument for a domestic
> design is not that it is quicker — it is that the licensed route caps you at exactly where Akkuyu is
> now: high supply localisation, no design ownership, no export right. If the goal is a fleet plus an
> export position, someone has to own a design. We are offering a fully worked one, with the neutronics,
> safety and cogeneration analysis behind it, open to review."

**"How many suppliers per unit?"**
> "We have not produced a component-level bill of materials, and our report names that as remaining work
> — I am not going to pretend otherwise. What we have is the major-component definition: one vessel, one
> integral steam generator, sixteen rod assemblies and drives, thirty-seven fuel assemblies, two passive
> heat-removal trains, one turbine-generator, one condenser, two diesel generators, thirteen buildings
> over about 4,800 square metres. The Akkuyu vendor list already covers most of the non-nuclear scope."

**"Is Baykar not already building a 40 MW SMR?"**
> "Reported, yes — and that is confirmation the power class is right, not a problem for us. We are a
> university design team offering a complete, reviewable detailed design in that class. Whoever
> industrialises it needs exactly the analysis basis we have built, and we would rather it were used than
> duplicated."

# ⚠ ACCURACY — WHAT IS SOLID AND WHAT IS OURS

**From our own FER, quote freely:** the §8.12.5 sentences on series production; $8,250/kWe as nth-of-a-kind
sitting above the vendor target; the ×0.55 and ×0.72 credits; the 0.6-power scaling chain; every
component count and building footprint; the removed-from-supply-chain list.

**Search-verified, safe to state:** Akkuyu at 56 % local content, 400+ firms, ~$7.5 bn; TSE as Material
Approval Organization since 7 Jan 2020; NDK independent since 2018; Authorizations Regulation in force
17 Mar 2023 with SMRs inside its scope; TNEAŞ–KEPCO MoU of 24 Nov 2025 covering licensing and financing;
Sinop decision expected in 2026 (AP1000 / APR1400 candidates); Thrace with SPIC CAP1400; the 5 GW SMR
target; the TENMAK/TÜBİTAK domestic reactor call.

**Reported — say "reported":** Baykar's 40 MW SMR prototype; IC Holding / ARC 20-unit partnership.

**Ours, label as a proposal:** the ≥ 70 % local content target, the Y1–Y5 ramp, the two-units-per-year
rate, the unit-5 domestic-forging and unit-3 I&C transition points, and the 12-unit / $4 bn threshold.
Arithmetic on the 5 GW target is solid; the *share we would win* is an assumption.

**Do not claim:** a costed factory, a supplier count per unit, or that five years beats Akkuyu's sixteen
— different clocks, and ours starts from a finished design.
