# Protocol Presentation — Build Guide

**Format:** ≤ 7.5 minutes · separate deck from the jury defence · 16:9
**Audience:** protokol — minister, TENMAK leadership, T3 Vakfı board, rectors, invited VIPs
**Purpose:** representational, not scored. Your technical marks come from the main defence.

---

## 1. Understand the genre before you build

This is **not a short version of the jury deck.** Cutting the 40-slide defence down to 8 produces the worst possible protocol presentation: dense, defensive, full of acronyms, and answering questions nobody in the room asked.

| | Jury defence | Protocol presentation |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Nuclear engineers | Officials, some technical, most not |
| They want | Is it correct? Is it defensible? | Why does this matter for Turkey? |
| Currency | Margins, benchmarks, criteria | Capability, jobs, energy, prestige |
| Failure mode | Unsupported claim | Losing the room in 30 seconds |
| Tone | Rigorous | Confident and plain |

A delegation moves down a line of teams. You may get less than your 7.5 minutes, you may be interrupted, and the minister may ask one question and move on. **Build so that the first 45 seconds work standalone.**

---

## 2. Language — decide this first

The audience is Turkish officials. Your team presents in English (established for the jury defence, where English is permitted).

**Recommendation: Turkish slides, English delivery.**

- Slide text **entirely in Turkish**, large and sparse — the officials read the slides
- You speak in **English**, slowly, matching the slide structure
- Technical terms stay international (MW, hidrojen, reaktör) — no need to invent Turkish for everything

This works because a protocol deck is visual: the slides carry ~70 % of the message. It also signals respect, which is much of the point of a protocol presentation.

**Better if you can get it:** ask a Turkish-speaking mentor, advisor or volunteer to deliver these 7.5 minutes while the team stands with the display. Protocol presentations are commonly delivered by whoever speaks best to the room — it is not a scored individual performance.

**Have the Turkish reviewed by a fluent technical speaker.** Same warning as the jury deck: a wrong term in front of a minister is worse than English.

---

## 3. Time and slide budget

**7.5 min = 450 seconds.** Allow 30 s of slack for interruption and greeting → build to **~420 s of content**.

| # | Slide | Time | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title / hero image | 0:20 | 0:20 |
| 2 | Why Turkey needs this | 1:00 | 1:20 |
| 3 | What Aegis-40 is | 1:15 | 2:35 |
| 4 | Why it is safe | 1:15 | 3:50 |
| 5 | More than electricity | 1:10 | 5:00 |
| 6 | Made in Turkey | 1:00 | 6:00 |
| 7 | Where we are now | 0:40 | 6:40 |
| 8 | Team + thank you | 0:30 | 7:10 |

**Eight slides. Do not exceed nine.** At this length every extra slide costs ~50 seconds you do not have.

## 4. Slide-by-slide specification

### Slide 1 — Title / hero · `0:20`

**Visual:** full-bleed reactor render. Use the RPV cutaway currently on jury slide 3 (`hero_rpv`, 2048×1991) — it is the single most impressive asset you own. Dark navy background, image right, text left.

**Text (Turkish, minimal):**
> **AEGIS-40**
> Yerli tasarım küçük modüler reaktör
> 125 MW(t) · 40 MW(e) · Sinop
> *Takım NUClearly · TEKNOFEST 2026*

**Say:** "Good morning. We are team NUClearly. This is Aegis-40 — a small modular reactor designed for the Turkish coast."

**Rule:** no logos larger than the reactor. No bullet points on a title slide.

---

### Slide 2 — Why Turkey needs this · `1:00`

The most important slide. Officials decide here whether to keep listening.

**Visual:** map of Turkey with Sinop marked, or three large icons (energy security / net-zero 2053 / domestic industry). **No charts.**

**Three lines, one idea each:**
> **Enerji güvenliği** — dışa bağımlılığı azaltan yerli üretim
> **2053 net-sıfır** — karbonsuz taban yük
> **Sanayi kabiliyeti** — SMR teknolojisinde yerli yetkinlik

**Say:** "Turkey imports most of its energy. Large reactors take a decade and cost billions. A small modular reactor is built in a factory, delivered to site, and starts producing years sooner — and it can be built with domestic industry."

**Do not** show LCOE, discount rates or capacity factors. The word "economics" is fine; the tornado chart is not.

---

### Slide 3 — What Aegis-40 is · `1:15`

**Visual:** labelled RPV cutaway (jury Fig. 1), with **only four labels** — core / steam generator / pressuriser / vessel. Strip every other annotation.

**Four large numbers across the bottom** — this is the slide people photograph:
> **125 MW(t)** termal güç · **40 MW(e)** elektrik · **6 yıl** yakıt değişimsiz · **60 yıl** ömür

**Say:** "Everything — the core, the steam generator, the pressuriser — sits inside one vessel. There are no large pipes to break, and no pumps: the water circulates by natural convection. It runs six years without refuelling."

**Optional impact line:** "≈ 125,000 hane" (households). ⚠️ **Verify before use** — computed from 316,236 MWh/yr sold at ~2,500 kWh per Turkish household. Check against a current TÜİK figure.

---

### Slide 4 — Why it is safe · `1:15`

Officials remember Fukushima and Chernobyl. Address it without naming them.

**Visual:** simple three-step graphic — *Elektrik kesilir → Sistem kendiliğinden devreye girer → 10 gün müdahalesiz soğuma*. Big arrows, no P&ID.

**Text:**
> **Pasif güvenlik** — elektrik, deniz suyu ve operatör müdahalesi olmadan **240 saatten fazla** soğuma
> **Borsuz aktif kalp** — normal işletmede suya kimyasal katılmaz; bir kaza sınıfı tasarımdan kaldırılır
> **Büyük boru yok** — en ciddi soğutucu kaybı kazası mimariyle elimine edilir

**Say:** "If the plant loses all power, nothing needs to be switched on. Gravity and natural circulation cool the reactor for more than ten days with no operator action. And because the coolant carries no boron chemistry, a whole class of accident simply does not exist in this design."

**Plain-language translation of your own jargon:**

| Never say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| soluble-boron-free, Gd₂O₃/Er₂O₃ | "the reactor is controlled without chemicals in the water" |
| MDNBR 1.33, F_ΔH | "large thermal safety margin" |
| CDF 10⁻⁸/ry | "the probability is vanishingly small" |
| passive residual heat removal, IRWST | "it cools itself" |
| once-through cycle | "sealed for six years" |

---

### Slide 5 — More than electricity · `1:10`

Your strongest differentiator for this audience — hydrogen and district heat are politically salient.

**Visual — must be made new.** One reactor icon on the left, three arrows right to three icons:
> ⚡ **Elektrik** 40 MW(e) → şebeke
> 🔥 **Bölgesel ısıtma** 25 MW(t) → şehir
> 💧 **Hidrojen** → sanayi ve ulaşım

**Do not reuse** `pfd_cogeneration_tces.png` or the site-layout block diagram (`hero_site`). Both are engineering drawings — correct for the jury, illegible and off-putting here.

**Say:** "The same reactor does three jobs. It makes electricity for the grid, heat for a nearby town, and when electricity demand is low it makes hydrogen instead of throttling down. One plant, three products."

---

### Slide 6 — Made in Turkey · `1:00`

Aim this squarely at the delegation's mandate.

**Visual:** four or five simple boxes marking domestic-capability areas — mühendislik ve modelleme · inşaat · dijital ikiz · TCES/H₂ · saha yerleşimi.

**Text:**
> Tasarımın büyük bölümü **yerli olarak yapılabilir**
> Açık kaynaklı araçlarla doğrulanmış — dışa bağımlı lisans yok
> **2053 net-sıfır hedefiyle uyumlu**

**Say:** "We designed this with open-source tools, so there is no foreign licence in the chain. Modelling, layout, civil works, the digital twin and the hydrogen island can all be done domestically. This is a capability, not just a design."

---

### Slide 7 — Where we are now · `0:40`

Credibility comes from **not** overclaiming. Officials have heard many student projects promise too much.

**Text:**
> **Tamamlanan:** tam detay tasarım, bağımsız doğrulama, tekrarlanabilir hesap paketi
> **Sıradaki:** yapısal nitelendirme, lisanslama öncesi analizler
> Bu bir **lisanslı santral tasarımı değil**, tutarlı ve doğrulanmış bir detay tasarımdır

**Say:** "We are not presenting a licensed plant. We are presenting a complete, independently checked detailed design — and we are explicit about what still has to be done."

That sentence buys more trust in this room than any number on any other slide.

---

### Slide 8 — Team and thanks · `0:30`

**Visual:** team photo if you have one, otherwise six names with roles. Institution logo. TEKNOFEST branding.

> **TEŞEKKÜRLER**
> Takım NUClearly — Aegis-40

**Say:** "Six of us built this. Thank you — we would be glad to answer any questions."

Then **stop talking.** Let them ask.

## 5. Style rules

**Palette** — reuse the project identity so the two decks look like one programme:
`navy #1b3d70` (primary) · `green #12905a` (safety/positive) · `orange #e0801e` (energy/accent) · `white`

**Typography**
- Headline **44–60 pt**
- Body **28 pt minimum** — if it does not fit at 28 pt, cut words, do not shrink type
- Big numbers **90–130 pt** — these are the slide, not decoration
- One typeface, two weights

**Density**
- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs "and", it is two slides.
- **≤ 20 words of body text per slide**, excluding the number callouts
- Every slide must be readable from 5 metres — a delegation stands back

**Images**
- Full-bleed or half-bleed, never a small thumbnail floating in white space
- Renders and photographs over diagrams; diagrams over charts; **no charts at all**

**Forbidden on every slide**
- Equations, Greek letters, subscripted symbols
- Code, tool names (OpenMC, OpenFOAM, DWSIM, Serpent)
- Uncertainty notation (±, σ), pcm, k_eff
- Reference numbers `[4,5]`
- Anything with the word "benchmark"

## 7. Delivery notes

- **Front-load.** If the delegation leaves after 90 seconds, slides 1–3 must have delivered the message. Never save the punchline.
- **Rehearse a 90-second version** (slides 1, 3, 5, 8) for the case where you are cut short. This *will* happen to someone in the line.
- **Rehearse the one-sentence version:** "A 40-megawatt Turkish small modular reactor that cools itself without power, runs six years without refuelling, and makes electricity, heat and hydrogen." Memorise it in both languages.
- **Do not read the slides.** They are in Turkish, you are speaking English — reading aloud will collide badly.
- **Assume a photograph.** Slide 3 will end up in someone's phone. Make it the one you would choose.
- **Have one person designated to answer.** VIP questions are usually one, short, and often about cost or safety. Prepare exactly two answers: *"is it safe?"* and *"what would it cost?"*

## 9. What this deck must not become

A compressed jury defence. If someone in the room could not repeat your main point to a colleague afterwards in one sentence, the deck has failed — regardless of how correct every number on it was.

The main point, for this audience, is:

> **Turkey can build a small nuclear plant that cools itself, runs six years without refuelling, and produces electricity, heat and hydrogen — and this team has designed it in detail.**

Everything on the eight slides should serve that sentence.
