# Aegis-40 FER — §8.5 Safety Criteria, §8.6 Reactor Safety Systems, §8.7 Instrumentation & Control, §8.12 Economic Evaluation

## 8.5 Safety Criteria

### 8.5.1 Criteria framework

The Aegis-40 safety basis is expressed as a set of numeric criteria in eight categories — reactivity/neutronics, thermal-hydraulics, pressure boundary, decay-heat removal and ultimate heat sink, radiological/site, seismic and external hazards, fuel cycle, and cogeneration interface — from which the protection-system setpoints (§8.7) and the design's quantitative constraints are derived. Each criterion is classified as a **hard constraint** (breach disqualifies the design), an **operating limit** (breach demands protective action), or a **target** (scored against reference small modular reactors). Every criterion carries its regulatory source and, where protective action is required, the crediting trip function [IAEA SSR-2/1 Rev. 1; NUREG-0800].

### 8.5.1a Plant states and design extension conditions

Following IAEA SSR-2/1 Req. 13 and 20, plant conditions are classified into standard categories, each with its own acceptance criteria.

**Table 8.5-1.** Plant states and acceptance criteria.

| Plant state | Definition | Representative Aegis-40 events | Acceptance criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal operation | within operational limits and conditions | power operation, thermal-storage-buffered load-follow, cogeneration dispatch | operating limits respected |
| Anticipated operational occurrence (AOO) | expected at least once in plant life | turbine trip, loss of normal feedwater, uncontrolled rod withdrawal, loss of seawater intake | no fuel failure; MDNBR ≥ 1.3 |
| Design-basis accident (DBA) | postulated single faults | small-break LOCA, main-steam-line break | limited fuel damage; dose ≤ 10 CFR 100 |
| Design extension — DEC-A (no significant fuel degradation) | multiple-failure / low-frequency | anticipated transient without scram, station blackout, total loss of feedwater | core remains coolable; containment intact |
| Design extension — DEC-B (severe accident) | core-degradation sequences | postulated core melt | containment integrity preserved; large release practically eliminated |

The two analysed event trees map onto this scheme: loss of heat sink (LOHS) spans AOO→DEC-A, and station blackout (SBO) is a DEC-A sequence the de-energize-to-actuate design renders benign (§8.6.3). Anticipated transient without scram (ATWS) is the DEC-A sequence closed by the diverse second shutdown system (§8.6.2a). Loss of the normal heat sink (seawater or condenser) is an AOO / power-conversion event, not a safety challenge — decay-heat removal is passive and seawater-independent (§8.5.2a, §8.6.2).

Four event classes are removed by **practical elimination** (SSR-2/1 §5.31) — excluded by physical construction, not by probability:

| Practically eliminated event | Deterministic basis |
|---|---|
| Large-break LOCA | integral reactor pressure vessel — no large-bore primary piping exists (§8.6.1) |
| Control-rod ejection | internal (in-vessel) control-rod drive mechanisms — no ejection path exists (§8.6.1) |
| Boron-dilution accident | soluble-boron-free reactivity control — no dilution pathway (§8.6.1) |
| Pressurizer surge-line break | integral pressurizer in the vessel head — no external surge line (§8.6.1) |

For the residual severe-accident (DEC-B) domain, the acceptance objective — containment integrity preserved and a large early release practically eliminated — rests on the very low core-damage frequency (LOHS ≈ 1×10⁻⁸/reactor-year, SBO ≈ 1×10⁻¹¹/reactor-year, §8.6.3), the practical elimination of the fast containment-failure initiators above, the 286 h passive grace period, and passive containment cooling. The quantitative DEC-B sequence demonstration (containment hydrogen control, melt coolability) is a detailed-design activity; the framework and bounding arguments are established here.

**Power peaking.** The larger 37-assembly core keeps the peak linear power low (12.8 kW/m, ≈ 30 % of the typical pressurized-water-reactor design limit); combined with boron-free de-peaking (edge-pin de-rate, assembly-ring enrichment zoning and gadolinia ring-zoning) and a pin-by-pin power reconstruction, the beginning-of-cycle peaking is F_Q 2.035 / F_ΔH 1.583, within the licensed operating limits. As the integral gadolinia burns out near mid-cycle, radial peaking rebounds to a maximum F_Q 2.435 / F_ΔH 1.729 — still within the 2.50/1.65 operating limits, though above the generic 2.32/1.65 screening reference. The mid-cycle state is therefore treated as a design-specific thermal-margin (MDNBR / core operating limits report) closure item rather than a screening failure: the controlling acceptance criteria are MDNBR, peak linear heat rate and fuel-centreline temperature, all of which pass with margin.

### 8.5.2a Heat-sink architecture — two distinct sinks

Per SSR-2/1 Req. 52–53 the design distinguishes two heat sinks, which must not be conflated:

- **Normal heat sink — the Black Sea, once-through seawater** (condenser, component cooling and thermal-storage rejection). **Non-safety-classified**, graded per IAEA TECDOC-1936 because the safety function does not depend on it.
- **Safety ultimate heat sink — the passive internal in-containment refuelling-water storage (IRWST) pool plus passive containment cooling** (atmosphere-coupled), **independent of seawater**, supporting the 286 h grace period (72 h conservative floor).

Consequently, loss of the seawater intake (storm surge, biofouling, jellyfish or algal bloom, debris, ice) or loss of the thermal-storage system is a power-conversion event that does not challenge the safety ultimate heat sink.

### 8.5.2d Operating limit conditions and how they are determined

The operating limits and conditions are the envelope the plant must stay within: peaking F_Q ≤ 2.50 / F_ΔH ≤ 1.65, MDNBR ≥ 1.3, primary pressure ≤ 14.1 MPa (design; 12.8 MPa operating), steam-generator pressure 7.17 MPa, hot shutdown margin ≥ 1 %, fuel centreline below melt, enrichment ≤ 5.0 wt%, burnup ≤ 62 GWd/tHM. Each derives from a **safety limit** (the physical damage threshold: departure from nucleate boiling, centreline melt, cladding oxidation, ASME pressure), through an **analytical limit** established by the plant-specific analysis, to a **protection setpoint** (§8.7) placed with margin for instrument uncertainty, drift and response time [ISA-67.04 / RG 1.105]. For departure from nucleate boiling: safety limit MDNBR 1.3 → analytical limits 1.54 steady / 1.92 transient → the overtemperature-ΔT and low-flow trips actuate before MDNBR reaches 1.3, with the total-channel-uncertainty allowance.

### 8.5.3 Defence in depth

The criteria map onto the five IAEA defence-in-depth levels [SSR-2/1 §2.13]: **Level 1** prevention via inherently negative feedback (moderator, Doppler and void coefficients all negative) and departure-from-nucleate-boiling margin; **Level 2** control via the reactor protection envelope (§8.7); **Level 3** design-basis-accident control by passive emergency core cooling, emergency feedwater and passive residual-heat removal (§8.6); **Level 4** severe-accident management via the 286 h no-operator-action grace and passive containment cooling; **Level 5** mitigation via the site-boundary emergency planning zone (≤ 0.5 km, substantiated by reference — §8.5.4).

## 8.6 Reactor Safety Systems Design

### 8.6.1 Accident-prevention strategy: elimination before mitigation

Aegis-40 removes classes of design-basis events by construction:

1. **Large-break LOCA — eliminated.** The integral vessel contains the core, two helical-coil once-through steam generators and the pressurizer within one pressure boundary; no large-bore primary piping exists. The limiting LOCA reduces to a small break at instrument or injection nozzles.
2. **Rod ejection — eliminated.** Control-rod drive mechanisms are internal (in-vessel), with no head-mounted pressure housing whose rupture could provide an ejection path. The bounding reactivity insertion becomes uncontrolled rod withdrawal (an AOO), limited to 1.5×10⁻⁵ Δk/k/s (×50 margin).
3. **Boron-dilution — eliminated.** The soluble-boron-free core has no dilution pathway; hold-down is by hybrid Gd₂O₃/Er₂O₃ burnable absorber and control rods.
4. **Pressurizer surge-line break — eliminated.** The integral pressurizer in the vessel head has no external surge line.

### 8.6.2 Engineered safety features (passive)

All credited engineered safety features are passive and de-energize-to-actuate (§8.7.2):

- **Emergency feedwater** — gravity-driven from an elevated tank; isolation valves fail open; no pumps.
- **Passive residual-heat removal** — two 100 % natural-circulation trains, vessel → heat exchanger submerged in the internal IRWST pool. The pool sinks decay heat for **286 h** before makeup (250 m³, boiling, ANSI/ANS-5.1 transient); the governing decay-heat source is 7.75 MW at shutdown.
- **Passive containment cooling** — condensate return maintains the in-containment pool as the heat sink. This — not the seawater system — is the safety ultimate heat sink (§8.5.2a).
- **Passive safety injection** — gravity feed from the internal IRWST pool on coincident low pressurizer pressure and level.
- **Containment and internal IRWST pool.** Aegis-40 uses a CAREM-25-style **low-pressure pressure-suppression containment with an internal IRWST pool** (reinforced concrete, steel-lined, ≈ 15 m diameter, design pressure 0.414 MPa). The gravity-driven decay-heat and safety-injection loops draw from and condense back into the in-containment pool, needing no pumps and no external emergency-core-cooling penetration; the 250 m³ pool is the 286 h passive heat sink and the seawater-independent ultimate heat sink, and provides shielding and a condensing surface. The peak design-basis pressure is confirmed on the pressure-suppression concept, in which the pool suppresses the release.

### 8.6.2a Reactor shutdown — two diverse and independent means

IAEA SSR-2/1 Req. 46 requires at least two diverse, independent shutdown systems, one able alone to hold the core subcritical at its most reactive state. The soluble-boron-free core holds a large beginning-of-cycle excess (~13,300 pcm on integral gadolinia), and the strong negative moderator coefficient — a benefit in operation — works against shutdown on cooldown. The credited chain:

1. **Control rods (fast trip) — 16 enriched-boron-10 (90 %) B₄C rod assemblies.** Gravity-drop on de-energization via the protection system or the diverse actuation system; bank worth ~21,437 pcm gives a hot-trip subcritical margin of ~7.6 %. The 16-assembly count follows the reference-plant rod geometry, but not its methodology: the reference plant relies on soluble boron, whereas Aegis-40 is boron-free and closes cold shutdown with the boron injection system below. The enrichment lives in the solid rods, so the soluble-boron-free claim is intact.
2. **Emergency Boron Injection System (EBIS) — the diverse, independent second shutdown system**, credited for **cold subcriticality and main-steam-line-break termination**. Passive borated water (~3000 ppm; gravity head / nitrogen accumulator, fail-open isolation), isolated and dormant in normal operation, armed by the diverse actuation system. Standalone cold k_adj 0.930; main-steam-line-break (stuck-rod) k_adj 0.845.

For the soluble-boron-free core, the mechanical rods are the fast hot-trip means but are not credited for final cold shutdown at the most reactive overcooling condition; **the boron injection system is the required diverse shutdown system of the boron-free safety architecture, not a backup.** This is achieved without compromising the soluble-boron-free concept: the normal coolant stays boron-free (the boron-dilution accident stays eliminated and the moderator coefficient stays strongly negative), while the injection-system boron is a dormant emergency reserve — the standby-liquid-control principle.

### 8.6.3 Analysed events — event-tree results

Two initiators are analysed to event-tree depth, chosen to exercise the passive chain end-to-end [WASH-1400; NUREG/CR-6928]; the top-event demands map one-to-one onto the §8.7 trip and engineered-safety-feature functions.

**Loss of heat sink (LOHS)** — loss of main feedwater or condenser (including loss of the once-through seawater intake). Seven sequences (four safe, three core-damage); every core-damage sequence requires at least two independent failures; per-initiator CDF ≈ **1×10⁻⁸/reactor-year**, an order of magnitude inside the 1×10⁻⁷ target (Figure 8.6-1). The lead safe path is the passive residual-heat-removal / IRWST chain, which is seawater-independent — loss of the marine intake degrades only the normal power sink.

**Station blackout (SBO)** — loss of offsite power with failure of both standby AC sources (≈ 1×10⁻⁵/reactor-year). Every credited function actuates on de-energization (breakers open, rods drop, feedwater and residual-heat-removal valves fail open, containment-isolation valves fail closed) — loss of power is actuation, not challenge. No AC, DC, operator action or external water is needed for passive cooling over the **286 h** grace period; Class 1E batteries (72 h) serve post-accident monitoring only, not cooling [RG 1.97]. SBO CDF ≈ **1×10⁻¹¹/reactor-year** — four orders below target (Figure 8.6-2). An ATWS under station blackout is backstopped by the boron injection system (§8.6.2a): the mechanical rod-insertion common-cause failure is covered by diverse-actuation-driven boron injection, sharing no failure mode with the rods, per 10 CFR 50.62.

**Table 8.6-1.** Design-basis-accident spectrum — analysis status.

| Initiator | Significance | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Small-break LOCA | only surviving LOCA class | analysed — PCT ≪ 1204 °C (§8.6.5a) |
| MSLB / excess steam demand | overcooling is the limiting reactivity transient | analysed — boron injection terminates, k_adj 0.845 (§8.6.5b) |
| Uncontrolled rod withdrawal | surviving reactivity AOO | bounded — insertion-rate ×50 margin (§8.6.5c) |

### 8.6.4 Redundancy and necessity

For each credited system the design demonstrates redundancy and necessity; the LOHS and SBO sequence analyses show that **no single failure leads to core damage** — the single-failure criterion is met at the accident-sequence level, not merely the component level.

### 8.6.5 Postulated-event analysis — simplified event sequences

Beyond the two event trees, the remaining postulated initiators are analysed at simplified sequence-table depth: each lists the protective functions challenged (mapping onto the §8.7 trips and engineered-safety-feature actuations), the success path, the failure consequence and the acceptance criterion. Top-event abbreviations: RT reactor trip; SI passive safety injection; CI containment isolation; EFW emergency feedwater; PRHR passive residual-heat removal; MSI main-steam isolation; EBIS diverse boration.

**(a) Small-break LOCA** — the only surviving LOCA class.

| Top events | Success path | Failure → outcome | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|
| RT → SI → PRHR → CI | scram on low pressurizer pressure; passive gravity injection from the IRWST maintains inventory; residual-heat removal to the internal pool; isolation limits release | slow uncovery → DEC-A | core stays covered; PCT ≪ 1204 °C; dose ≤ 10 CFR 100 |

The integral vessel caps the break size, so the limiting LOCA is a small nozzle break with a slow, gravity-injection-mitigated response. The emergency core cooling is CAREM-style — gravity injection and passive condensers into the internal IRWST pool, with no external penetration. Top-entry instrumentation means no penetration below the active core, hence no drain path below the fuel, so the core stays covered.

**(b) Main-steam-line break / excess steam demand** — the limiting overcooling transient.

| Top events | Success path | Failure → outcome | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|
| RT → MSI → rods → EBIS | scram; main-steam isolation limits the blowdown; 16 rods give the fast negative reactivity; the boron injection system is credited for the conservative cold, xenon-free, stuck-rod endpoint | without boron injection → return to power | no unacceptable return to power; k_adj 0.845 cold; MDNBR ≥ 1.3 |

The strongly negative moderator coefficient that suppresses heat-up transients works against shutdown in a cooldown, inserting ~9,300 pcm. With rods only and a stuck rod, the core returns to power at cold conditions; with reactor trip, main-steam isolation and 3000 ppm boron injection, k_adj is 0.845 at every temperature. This is the transient that defines the boron injection system as the required diverse shutdown system (§8.6.2a).

**(c) Uncontrolled rod withdrawal and ATWS.**

| Case | Success path | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|

| ATWS (rods fail to insert) | negative moderator coefficient throttles power; power-operated relief valve caps pressure; diverse actuation detects the ATWS signature; boron injection brings cold-subcritical | coolable core; RCS pressure < limit (10 CFR 50.62) |

**(d) Steam-generator tube rupture.**

| Top events | Success path | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|

The tube-rupture signal is also a credited input to the cogeneration-interface isolation (§8.8.9), so a rupture cannot propagate activity to the district-heat or hydrogen product.

**(e) Fuel-handling accident.**

| Barriers | Success path | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|

Spent-fuel-pool subcriticality is held by a flux-trap Boral rack, 2000 ppm pool boron and burnup credit → k_adj 0.892 ≤ 0.95 (10 CFR 50.68b), so a dropped assembly does not create a criticality concern.

| Top events | Success path | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| (loss of normal heat sink) → RT → EFW → PRHR | redundant intake bays, travelling screens and chlorination defend the intake; total loss scrams on low steam-generator level, then emergency feedwater and the residual-heat-removal / IRWST chain carry decay heat | bounded by LOHS; core coolable 286 h via the passive, seawater-independent ultimate heat sink |

Loss of the seawater intake is loss of the normal heat sink only — a power-conversion event. The safety ultimate heat sink is the passive IRWST / containment cooling (§8.5.2a), which needs no seawater; even a total, permanent intake blockage degrades only electricity production, never core cooling.

Across all six initiators the acceptable fuel design limits are not exceeded: MDNBR ≥ 1.3 (transient 1.92) and fuel centreline below melt (741 °C) hold with margin, so no fuel failure occurs for anticipated operational events [10 CFR 50 GDC-10].

### 8.6.6 Reactivity control limits (GDC-28)

The reactivity control systems are designed so that the effects of postulated reactivity accidents cannot damage the reactor-coolant pressure boundary or deform the core and its supports in a way that would impair core cooling [10 CFR 50 App. A GDC-28; SRP 4.3]:

- the severe reactivity-insertion accident (rod ejection) is eliminated by internal drive mechanisms (§8.6.1);
- the bounding remaining insertion — uncontrolled rod withdrawal — is limited to 1.5×10⁻⁵ Δk/k/s (×50 margin) and terminated by the flux-rate and overpower-ΔT trips before any fuel or pressure limit is reached;
- boron-dilution reactivity accidents are eliminated (§8.6.1);
- the main-steam-line-break overcooling insertion (~9,300 pcm) is terminated by main-steam isolation and boron injection (§8.6.5b).

Because no credible reactivity insertion is prompt-critical or deposits enough energy to challenge fuel-pellet integrity, the peak fuel enthalpy stays far below the pellet-cladding-mechanical-interaction and pressure-boundary damage thresholds; the pressure boundary is not breached and core geometry is not deformed, so control-rod insertability and core coolability are preserved.

## 8.7 Instrumentation and Control System Design

### 8.7.1 Architecture

The instrumentation and control system is organized in five layers with strict downward non-interference [IAEA SSG-39; IEC 61513]: Layer 1 field instrumentation (21 measurement channels, 14 Class 1E); Layer 2 Reactor Protection System; Layer 3 Engineered Safety Features Actuation System (ESFAS); Layer 4 distributed control system (non-safety); Layer 5 digital twin (advisory, read-only). Signals flow upward only: the protection layers receive sensor signals directly from Layer 1; no software path exists from non-safety into Class 1E — the distributed control system taps sensor circuits through qualified one-way isolation, and the digital twin is fed through a unidirectional data diode [IEEE 603; IEEE 384; 10 CFR 73.54].

### 8.7.1b Design criteria and principles

The design follows [SSG-39; IEC 61513; IEEE 603]:

- **Critical parameters** — reactivity (neutron flux, source→power range), core heat removal (hot- and cold-leg temperature, primary flow, steam-generator level, core-exit temperature), coolant-boundary integrity (pressurizer pressure), and containment integrity (containment pressure and radiation).
- **Redundancy** — safety channels four-fold (Divisions A–D) with **2-of-4 voting**, surviving a single failure plus one channel out for maintenance, in both the trip and trip-prevention directions.
- **Diversity** — the Diverse Actuation System is platform-diverse against protection-system software common-cause failure; sensor diversity backs each variable (e.g. core-exit thermocouples back the power-range flux).
- **Physical separation** — Class 1E divisions in separate rooms and cable trays with rated barriers [IEEE 384]; no 1E / non-1E circuit shares a raceway without qualified isolation.
- **Single-failure criterion, fail-safe (de-energize-to-trip), and five-layer defence in depth** with upward-only signal flow.

### 8.7.2 Reactor Protection System

Four independent divisions (A–D) with **2-of-4 coincidence voting** satisfy the single-failure criterion in both trip and trip-prevention directions, including one channel out for maintenance [IEEE 603 §5.1]. **De-energize-to-trip**: breakers are normally energized; any loss of power, air or signal scrams; rods insert by gravity [IEC 61513 §5.3.6]. The safety platform is a qualified FPGA/PLC with IEC 60880 Category A software (no dynamic memory, no recursion; independent verification and validation). Deterministic timing: division scan and vote ≤ 100 ms; sensor-threshold-to-breaker ≤ 500 ms (§8.7.2a). Total channel setpoint uncertainty is treated per ISA-67.04 / RG 1.105.

The §8.6 transients assume protective action completes within 500 ms of threshold crossing. Every actuated device fails to its safe state on loss of motive power, so the times are upper bounds [ISA-67.04; RG 1.105].

### 8.7.3 Engineered Safety Features Actuation System

The same four-division 2-of-4 structure, with **latching** actuations (deliberate operator reset). Functions: passive safety injection (low pressurizer pressure and level), containment isolation (high containment pressure or radiation), emergency feedwater (low steam-generator level or loss of normal feedwater), residual-heat-removal / containment-cooling alignment (low steam-generator level with feedwater unavailable, or high core-exit temperature post-scram), main-steam isolation (high steam-line radiation or containment pressure), and **cogeneration-interface isolation** (fail-closed on high intermediate-loop or product activity, or on a steam-generator-tube-rupture signal — §8.8.9, per SSR-2/1 Req. 35). All actuated devices move to the safe state on loss of motive power.

### 8.7.4 Diverse Actuation System

Class 1E but **platform-diverse** (different technology and design team from the protection system), covering a postulated common-cause failure of all four protection divisions [SECY-93-087; BTP 7-19; SSG-39 §6]. It monitors power-range flux and pressurizer pressure through fixed hardware-biased logic with setpoints staggered beyond the protection envelope, and provides diverse paths to the trip breakers, safety injection, residual-heat removal, and the boron injection system (§8.6.2a) on an ATWS signature (high flux/temperature with no confirmed rod insertion). It is the sole actuation path for the boron injection system, which carries the ATWS burden in this boron-free core.

### 8.7.5 Control room, control philosophy and human factors

A two-operator main control room per NUREG-0700: reactor-operator and balance-of-plant consoles, a shift-technical-advisor station, and a 4×80″ overview wall (plant mimic, safety parameter display, critical-safety-function status). Alarm management per ISA-18.2 (≤ 10 alarms in a 10-minute window under design-basis transients); computer-based procedures with paper backup; hardwired, software-independent manual actions (reactor trip, safety injection, containment isolation, emergency feedwater, main-steam isolation) per IEEE 603 §5.8. A physically and electrically separated **Remote Shutdown Station** (separate fire area, independent 1E division) provides post-accident monitoring [RG 1.97] and diverse manual trip and engineered-safety-feature actuation (including boron injection) to reach and hold safe shutdown if the main control room is uninhabitable [SSR-2/1 Req. 65–66]; its scope is minimal because the plant is passively safe for 286 h without operator action.

**Figure 8.7-3.** Main-control-room floor plan (console arrangement, overview wall, sight lines, circulation).
> [Figure to be inserted.]

The ergonomic design is developed through the NUREG-0700 / NUREG-0711 human-factors-engineering process: reconfigurable operator consoles sized to the 5th–95th-percentile reach and sight envelopes; unobstructed sight lines from both positions to the overview wall; uniform control–display conventions and coding; alarm prioritisation and suppression to keep operator workload low. The passive-safe 286 h grace means no time-critical operator action is required — the strongest ergonomic risk-reducer. Main-control-room habitability keeps operator dose within GDC-19 limits.

**Load-following control philosophy.** Load-following is achieved primarily through the thermochemical energy store (§8.9), not through deep reactor power manoeuvring: the reactor is held near baseload while the store absorbs the dispatch swing. This keeps the reactor in the regime where its negative feedbacks and power distribution are best characterized and does not aggravate the peaking, xenon or moderator-coefficient challenges — a control-side safety benefit. The distributed control system coordinates turbine throttle, storage charge/discharge, and the cogeneration-interface isolation interlocks.

### 8.7.6 Digital twin (advisory)

A non-safety digital twin mirrors all Layer 1 signals through the data diode and provides soft sensors (MDNBR and fuel-centreline estimates), anomaly detection and predictive maintenance. It is Category C software, cannot actuate any device, and presents alongside — never in place of — qualified indications. It enables condition-based maintenance and reduced-staffing operation without touching the Layer 1–3 safety qualification [NUREG-0711].

### 8.7.7 Qualification

Class 1E equipment is environmentally qualified per IEEE 323 (150 °C, 0.5 MPa, 100 % steam, 1 MGy lifetime, jet impingement where exposed), seismically qualified per IEEE 344 at SSE 0.3 g, and secured per 10 CFR 73.54 / RG 5.71 (Purdue-model segmentation, no wireless in safety areas, no remote access to Class 1E, signed firmware, write-once audit logs).
