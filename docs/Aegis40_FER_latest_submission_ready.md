# **1. PROJECT ABSTRACT / SCOPE OF THE PROJECT**

## **1.1 Project Abstract**

Aegis-40 is a **40 MWe / 125 MWth integral pressurized-water small
modular reactor** developed for the **TEKNOFEST 2026 Detailed Design -
40 MWe Modular PWR** category. The design combines a 37-fuel-assembly,
17x17, 2.0 m active-height PWR core with natural-circulation primary
cooling, an integral once-through steam generator, passive safety
systems and a compact three-island plant arrangement.

The reactor is soluble-boron-free during normal operation. Excess
reactivity and power shape are controlled by Gd2O3/Er2O3 burnable
absorber zoning, enrichment grading and 16 control-rod assemblies. A
dormant Emergency Boron Injection System is retained only as a diverse
shutdown and accident-management layer, not as routine chemical shim.

The nuclear safety case is separated from the non-electric applications.
Black Sea seawater is used only as the normal balance-of-plant condenser
heat sink. Credited safety heat removal is provided by the
in-containment refuelling water storage tank (IRWST) and passive
containment-cooling path. Thermochemical energy storage and hydrogen
production are non-safety balance-of-plant / industrial-island systems;
their isolation or failure does not actuate or replace reactor
protection, shutdown, decay-heat-removal or confinement functions.

The project is presented as a standards-led detailed-design study with
an auditable analysis workflow covering neutronics, thermal-hydraulics,
fuel and materials, safety, instrumentation and control, auxiliary
systems, layout, waste management and economics. The advisory digital
twin supports review, training and diagnostics only; it remains
read-only and independent of the safety-class protection architecture.

## **1.2 Scope of the Project**

The FER scope follows the official Detailed Design - 40 MWe Modular PWR
headings. It covers plant description, core design, fuel and materials,
cooling circuits, safety criteria, reactor safety systems,
instrumentation and control, auxiliary systems, TCES and hydrogen
integration, facility layout, nuclear waste management, economic
evaluation, references and digital-appendix evidence for reproducibility
and benchmarking.

# **3. LITERATURE REVIEW**

Integral pressurized-water reactor small modular reactors provide the
main architectural reference for Aegis-40 because major primary-system
components are placed inside the reactor pressure vessel, reducing large
external primary piping and supporting compact reactor-building layouts.
NuScale-class and CAREM-class iPWR concepts are used as engineering
references for a 37-fuel-assembly-class core envelope,
natural-circulation cooling, passive heat removal and compact
containment arrangements (IAEA, n.d.-a; U.S. Nuclear Regulatory
Commission \[NRC\], 2023a). Aegis-40 uses these concepts as reference
anchors while developing its own soluble-boron-free reactivity-control
strategy.

Soluble-boron-free PWR studies define the main core-design challenge.
Eliminating soluble boron can simplify normal chemistry control and
remove the classical boron-dilution accident pathway, but it increases
the importance of burnable-absorber zoning, enrichment zoning, rod
worth, shutdown margin and local peaking control. Recent SBF SMR studies
emphasize that excess reactivity must be held down without unacceptable
power peaking or loss of shutdown capability (Kim et al., 2021).
Aegis-40 responds with ring-zoned gadolinia for early-life reactivity
control, erbia for slower residual hold-down, enrichment grading, 16
control-rod assemblies and dormant EBIS diversity.

Transparent reactor-physics methodology is central to the design basis.
OpenMC is suitable for a detailed-design study because it supports
continuous-energy Monte Carlo particle transport, modern nuclear-data
libraries, statistical uncertainty reporting, convergence checks and
reproducible model inputs (OpenMC Development Team, n.d.). BEAVRS-type
PWR benchmarks are relevant because they provide detailed full-core
information for loading patterns, burnable absorbers, detector data,
control rods and operating history (MIT Computational Reactor Physics
Group, n.d.). Therefore, the Aegis-40 neutronic evidence is not limited
to k-effective values; it also includes BOC/MOC/EOC power maps, absorber
burn-out behavior, feedback coefficients, shutdown-margin cases and
thermal-margin interfaces.

Long-cycle SMR cores commonly require large beginning-of-cycle excess
reactivity, compensated by burnable absorbers, soluble boron, control
rods or other reactivity-management features. Akbari-Jeyhouni et
al. (2018) showed for a SMART-based integral PWR study that long-cycle
SMR cores can have high beginning-of-cycle multiplication factors when
absorber and fuel-management choices are varied. This supports the
interpretation that high fresh-core reactivity is not a design failure
by itself; the relevant question is whether reactivity is acceptably
controlled while preserving negative feedback, shutdown margin,
power-shape control and thermal margin.

Thermal-hydraulic methodology is based on accepted light-water-reactor
practice: IAPWS-IF97 water/steam properties, CHF/DNBR correlations,
Dittus-Boelter heat transfer, Jens-Lottes subcooled-boiling treatment,
ANSI/ANS-5.1 decay heat and grid-convergence logic. Aegis-40 combines
natural-circulation loop balance, hot-channel correlation analysis and
secondary-cycle simulation to demonstrate primary cooling, OTSG
feasibility, Rankine-cycle performance and normal condenser heat
rejection.

Nuclear cogeneration literature supports the coupling of reactors to
district heat, process heat, desalination or hydrogen production when
the non-electric applications are isolated from reactor safety functions
(IAEA, n.d.-b; IAEA, 2016a). In Aegis-40, TCES and hydrogen systems are
therefore treated as non-safety balance-of-plant / industrial-island
modules. They receive secondary-side heat or electricity through
isolated interfaces and are not credited for reactor protection,
shutdown, containment isolation or decay heat removal.

Waste, safeguards and spent-fuel storage literature informs the back-end
section. The once-through fuel cycle is evaluated using
depletion-derived isotopics, decay heat, storage criticality criteria,
fission-product source terms and plutonium-vector indicators. Waste and
safeguards claims are therefore tied to the heavy-metal inventory,
burnup basis, isotope uncertainties, decay time and storage
configuration rather than stated as qualitative assertions.

## **4.2 Feasibility**

Aegis-40 is feasible as a conceptual detailed-design study because its
main engineering basis remains within established light-water reactor
technology. The design uses UO2 fuel, Zircaloy-4 cladding, a 17x17
fuel-assembly lattice, light-water coolant/moderator, an integral PWR
vessel, natural-circulation primary cooling and passive decay-heat
removal. The main design risk is therefore integration and optimization,
not the introduction of a new fuel type, coolant, moderator or
safety-class digital control concept.

The 37-fuel-assembly core is deliberately low-power-density. At 125 MWth
and approximately 9.39 tHM, the specific power is about 13.31 MW/tHM.
This lowers linear heat rate, reduces fuel-temperature demand, slows
burnup and supports the thermal-margin closure needed for
soluble-boron-free operation. Natural circulation is credible at this
scale because the integral steam generator is above the core and
provides the required thermal-center height.

TCES and hydrogen systems improve operational flexibility without
imposing rapid maneuvering on the reactor core. They are non-safety
balance-of-plant / industrial-island modules; their isolation, trip or
shutdown does not affect the credited nuclear safety functions.

Aegis-40 is not a licensed plant design. The FER is a detailed-design
competition study that demonstrates an internally consistent concept,
identifies margins, defines analysis methods and states the assumptions
that would require licensing-grade confirmation in later phases,
including structural qualification, detailed fuel performance,
containment response, site-specific dose assessment and
construction-level cost validation.

# **5. ORIGINALITY, INNOVATION AND DOMESTICITY OF THE PROJECT**

## **5.1 Originality / Innovative Aspects**

Aegis-40 combines three originality pillars into one 40 MWe iPWR
concept: soluble-boron-free normal operation, a low-power-density
long-cycle core, and non-safety polygeneration through the balance of
plant.

First, the reactor applies soluble-boron-free normal operation to an
integral PWR core. Routine reactivity management is shifted from
chemical shim to Gd2O3/Er2O3 absorber zoning, enrichment grading and 16
control-rod assemblies. EBIS remains only as a dormant diverse shutdown
and accident-management system. This preserves a conservative emergency
option without reintroducing soluble-boron chemistry into normal
operation.

Second, the core is intentionally low-power-density. Instead of
maximizing discharge burnup, the 37-FA layout prioritizes thermal
margin, low peak linear heat rate, long uninterrupted operation and
compatibility with passive safety. This makes the SBF strategy more
defensible because local peaking is closed through design-specific MDNBR
and fuel-temperature margins rather than by routine coolant boron.

Third, plant flexibility is moved to the non-safety balance of plant.
TCES district/process heat and off-peak hydrogen production allow the
secondary side to absorb demand variation while the nuclear island
remains focused on stable heat generation and safety. These systems
increase value but are not credited for protection, shutdown,
containment isolation or decay heat removal.


Fourth, the safety case uses a compact pressure-suppression containment
coupled to a 250 m3 internal IRWST/passive water inventory. At FER
stage the containment concept is supported by plant-specific
mass-and-energy screening for full-primary-inventory blowdown and by
published SMART100/CAREM/AP1000 reference evidence. This makes the
IRWST a load-bearing safety feature for suppression, gravity injection
and passive heat rejection rather than only a storage tank.

The advisory digital twin adds a digital-engineering layer without
becoming part of the safety actuation path. It visualizes selected
neutronic, thermal-hydraulic, safety, waste, economic and
mechanical-health indicators for review, training and diagnostics while
preserving the independence of the safety-class I&C architecture.

## **5.2 Domesticity Aspects**

The domesticity strategy is staged and realistic. In the near term,
Aegis-40 supports domestic capability in reactor-physics modelling,
thermal-hydraulic analysis, CAD-based layout, balance-of-plant design,
seawater heat-rejection engineering, digital engineering, TCES/H2
integration, safety documentation and non-safety modular construction.
These areas can be developed earlier than nuclear fuel fabrication,
reactor internals, nuclear pressure-boundary manufacturing or
safety-class I&C, which require longer qualification under the NDK
authorization, inspection and review framework.

Aegis-40 aligns with Türkiye’s national energy planning. The Türkiye
National Energy Plan is based on the 2053 net-zero target and projects
electricity consumption of 510.5 TWh by 2035, with nuclear installed
capacity reaching 7.2 GW (Republic of Türkiye Ministry of Energy and
Natural Resources, 2022). A 40 MWe modular PWR provides a design pathway
for firm low-carbon electricity, district/process heat and hydrogen
production in applications that may not require gigawatt-scale units.

The hydrogen interface is also consistent with Türkiye’s Hydrogen
Technologies Strategy and Roadmap, which targets 2 GW of electrolyzer
capacity by 2030, 5 GW by 2035 and 70 GW by 2053 while emphasizing
domestic electrolyzer components, system-control hardware/software and
testing infrastructure (Republic of Türkiye Ministry of Energy and
Natural Resources, 2023). Aegis-40 links steady nuclear heat generation
with off-peak hydrogen production through a non-safety industrial
interface.

The localization claim is therefore limited to a credible pathway. Early
domestic participation is most suitable for civil structures, modular
buildings, non-safety piping, HVAC, fire protection, conventional
electrical systems, radiation-monitoring equipment, digital dashboards,
TCES modules and hydrogen balance-of-plant equipment. Nuclear fuel,
cladding, reactor internals, pressure-boundary components, qualified
valves and safety-class I&C remain long-qualification items.

## **8.1 Design Preparation Phase and General Description of the Facility**

### **8.1.0 General description**

Aegis-40 is an integral, soluble-boron-free pressurized-water small
modular reactor rated 125 MWth / 40 MWe net. The core, helical-coil
once-through steam generator, self-pressurizer, control-rod drive
interfaces and major primary-system components are arranged inside a
single reactor pressure vessel. Primary coolant circulation is by
natural circulation, so the design has no reactor coolant pumps and no
large external primary loops.

The plant is arranged as a nuclear island, conventional island and
non-electric application island. The nuclear island contains the reactor
module, containment/IRWST passive heat sink, safety systems, spent-fuel
handling and controlled-access areas. The conventional island converts
steam to electricity and rejects normal heat to the Black Sea seawater
system. The non-electric application island contains TCES
district/process-heat equipment and hydrogen systems, isolated from the
nuclear safety case.

Black Sea seawater is the normal condenser and balance-of-plant heat
sink. It is not credited as the safety ultimate heat sink. The credited
safety ultimate heat sink is the internal IRWST pool and passive
containment-cooling path described in Sections 8.5 and 8.6.

### **8.1.5 Outage schedule and derived relations**

The Aegis-40 core is loaded once and operates for a single once-through
cycle of approximately 2224 EFPD, corresponding to 6.09 full-power years
at the STAT_FINAL depletion basis. There is no batch refuelling in the
baseline design. Planned unavailability is set by maintenance,
inspection and balance-of-plant outage requirements rather than periodic
fuel shuffling. At the end of cycle, the module is shut down and the
whole core is discharged.

Derived values are: net efficiency = 40/125 = 32.0%; specific power =
125/9.39 = 13.31 MW/tHM; primary flow from Q = m_dot c_p DeltaT gives
m_dot approximately 467 kg/s using c_p approximately 5.35 kJ/kg-K at 283
deg C and 12.8 MPa; and the record once-through discharge burnup is 29.6
GWd/tHM.

Future multi-batch fuel management is retained only as an extension. The
3-batch linear-reactivity-model option gives approximately 44.4 GWd/tHM
discharge burnup, but it would require a separate refuelling strategy,
peaking analysis, absorber re-optimization and fuel-performance
confirmation. It is not part of the baseline safety, waste or economic
case.

### **8.1.9 Mechanical and Structural Design Basis**

The reactor pressure vessel is the main structural and
pressure-retaining boundary of the nuclear steam supply system. It
houses the 37-FA core, lower support structure, flow distributor, core
barrel, water-reflector/downcomer region, integral helical-coil OTSG,
top-entry instrumentation, and the integrated self-pressurizer /
internal-hydraulic-CRDM upper package. The integral arrangement removes
reactor coolant pumps, large external primary loops, an external
pressurizer surge line and external rod-drive pressure housings.

This section records the CAD-controlled mechanical envelope and load
path only. Detailed pressure-boundary and support-skirt screening
calculations are given in Section 8.3.6; natural-circulation flow-area
implications are treated in Section 8.4; building, foundation and piping
interfaces are treated in Section 8.10.

#### **8.1.9.1 Mechanical load path**

Primary mechanical load path: fuel assemblies -\> lower support plate /
distributor -\> core barrel -\> RPV shell -\> support skirt -\> Seismic
Category I base mat.

The lower support and distributor locate the core and shape the inlet
flow. The core barrel separates the hot riser and cold downcomer regions
while maintaining fuel-assembly alignment. The OTSG is located above the
core so that the heat sink is vertically above the heat source,
supporting natural circulation. The self-pressurizer and internal
hydraulic CRDMs are arranged as a common upper-vessel package: the CRDM
guide columns extend through the self-pressurizer volume, while the
surrounding upper-plenum steam/water inventory provides the
self-pressurizer function. This avoids external rod-drive pressure
housings and removes the conventional rod-ejection path.

### **8.2.3 Neutronic analysis**

The neutronic design uses OpenMC 0.15.3 continuous-energy Monte Carlo
with ENDF/B-VIII.0 nuclear data on the full 3-D 37-FA Aegis-40 core. The
STAT_FINAL record run used 400 batches, 80 inactive batches and 50,000
particles per batch, giving approximately 16 million active particle
histories and a k-effective statistical uncertainty of about 22-26 pcm.
The locked model contains 37 FAs, 17x17 lattice, 2.0 m active height,
ring enrichment of 4.95/4.70/4.40/4.0 wt% U-235, 6 wt% Gd2O3 burnable
absorber rods with ring grading, 0.75 wt% Er2O3 rods, 16 CRAs with 90%
enriched B-10 absorber, 9.39 tHM and 125 MWth.

The record run confirms negative moderator, Doppler and void
coefficients; large rod-bank worth; shutdown margin exceeding the 1%
Delta-k/k target; and peak enrichment within the 5.0 wt% LEU envelope.
The once-through depletion calculation gives a k=1 cycle length of 2224
EFPD and a core-average discharge burnup of 29.6 GWd/tHM.

**Peaking-factor interpretation and design-specific closure**

The values F_deltaH \<= 1.65 and Fq \<= 2.32 are used in this FER as
conservative generic PWR screening references, not universal design
limits. In PWR practice, peaking limits are connected to plant-specific
thermal limits, core monitoring, operating procedures and the Core
Operating Limits Report. Therefore, an excess of a generic peaking
reference is not by itself a fuel-failure conclusion. The controlling
acceptance criteria are MDNBR, peak linear heat rate, fuel centerline
temperature, peak cladding temperature and accident-analysis
assumptions.

The STAT_FINAL cycle-peaking calculation shows that BOC and EOC remain
within the generic screening values, while the MOC Gd-burnout hump
exceeds them. The MOC state is therefore evaluated as a
neutronic-thermal-hydraulic interface case. The design-specific
hot-channel basis uses the MOC peaking envelope, F_deltaH = 1.75 and Fq
approximately 2.45 with the corresponding axial shape. The MDNBR, linear
heat rate and fuel/cladding temperature margins remain acceptable, so
Aegis-40 applies a design-specific COLR peaking envelope for the 37-FA
SBF core.

### 8.3.6 Reactor-vessel mechanical screening

The reactor-vessel mechanical screening is limited to the

### **8.4.1 Primary cooling - natural circulation**

The primary cooling loop is internal to the RPV. Hot water leaves the
core, rises through the central riser, turns into the upper OTSG region,
transfers heat to the helical-tube secondary side, descends through the
annular downcomer and returns to the lower plenum. The OTSG is placed
above the core so that the thermal centre of the heat sink is above the
core heat source. This elevation difference provides the buoyancy head
for natural circulation and removes the need for reactor coolant pumps.

The adopted design point is 125 MWth, 12.8 MPa, 258 deg C core inlet,
308 deg C core outlet, 50 K core temperature rise, approximately 467
kg/s primary flow and mass flux about 542-543 kg/m2-s. The core-to-OTSG
thermal-centre height is approximately 4.0 m, giving a driving head near
3.67 kPa. These values are consistent with the CAD elevation register,
the updated RPV arrangement and the OpenFOAM hot-channel boundary
conditions.

### **8.4.2 Integral OTSG and primary-secondary coupling**

## **8.5 Safety Criteria**

### 8.5.1 Criteria framework

The Aegis-40 safety basis is expressed as a set of numeric criteria in
eight categories - reactivity/neutronics, thermal-hydraulics, pressure
boundary, decay-heat removal and ultimate heat sink, radiological/site,
seismic and external hazards, fuel cycle, and cogeneration interface -
from which the protection-system setpoints (§8.7) and the design’s
quantitative constraints are derived. Each criterion is classified as a
**hard constraint** (breach disqualifies the design), an **operating
limit** (breach demands protective action), or a **target** (scored
against reference small modular reactors). Every criterion carries its
regulatory source and, where protective action is required, the
crediting trip function \[IAEA SSR-2/1 Rev. 1; NUREG-0800\].

### 8.5.1a Plant states and design extension conditions

Following IAEA SSR-2/1 Req. 13 and 20, plant conditions are classified
into standard categories, each with its own acceptance criteria.

**Table 8.5-1.** Plant states and acceptance criteria.

| Plant state                                                | Definition                               | Representative Aegis-40 events                                                               | Acceptance criterion                                                  |
|------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Normal operation                                           | within operational limits and conditions | power operation, thermal-storage-buffered load-follow, cogeneration dispatch                 | operating limits respected                                            |
| Anticipated operational occurrence (AOO)                   | expected at least once in plant life     | turbine trip, loss of normal feedwater, uncontrolled rod withdrawal, loss of seawater intake | no fuel failure; MDNBR >= 1.3                                          |
| Design-basis accident (DBA)                                | postulated single faults                 | small-break LOCA, main-steam-line break                                                      | limited fuel damage; dose <= 10 CFR 100                                |
| Design extension - DEC-A (no significant fuel degradation) | multiple-failure / low-frequency         | anticipated transient without scram, station blackout, total loss of feedwater               | core remains coolable; containment intact                             |
| Design extension - DEC-B (severe accident)                 | core-degradation sequences               | postulated core melt                                                                         | containment integrity preserved; large release practically eliminated |

The two analyzed event trees map onto this scheme: loss of heat sink
(LOHS) spans AOO->DEC-A, and station blackout (SBO) is a DEC-A sequence
the de-energize-to-actuate design renders benign (§8.6.3). Anticipated
transient without scram (ATWS) is the DEC-A sequence closed by the
diverse second shutdown system (§8.6.2a). Loss of the normal heat sink
(seawater or condenser) is an AOO / power-conversion event, not a safety
challenge - decay-heat removal is passive and seawater-independent
(§8.5.2a, §8.6.2).

Four event classes are removed by **practical elimination** (SSR-2/1
§5.31) - excluded by physical construction, not by probability:

| Practically eliminated event | Deterministic basis                                                                  |
|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Large-break LOCA             | integral reactor pressure vessel - no large-bore primary piping exists (§8.6.1)      |
| Control-rod ejection         | internal (in-vessel) control-rod drive mechanisms - no ejection path exists (§8.6.1) |
| Boron-dilution accident      | soluble-boron-free reactivity control - no dilution pathway (§8.6.1)                 |
| Pressurizer surge-line break | integral pressurizer in the vessel head - no external surge line (§8.6.1)            |

For the residual severe-accident (DEC-B) domain, the acceptance
objective - containment integrity preserved and a large early release
practically eliminated - rests on the very low core-damage frequency
(LOHS about  1x10⁻⁸/reactor-year, SBO about  1x10⁻¹¹/reactor-year, §8.6.3), the
practical elimination of the fast containment-failure initiators above,
the conservative passive grace period of at least 240 h, with a nominal
estimate of about 286 h, and passive containment cooling. The
quantitative DEC-B sequence demonstration (containment hydrogen control,
melt coolability) is a detailed-design activity; the framework and
bounding arguments are established here.

MDNBR, peak linear heat rate and fuel-centreline temperature, all of
which pass with margin.

### 8.5.2a Heat-sink architecture - two distinct sinks

Per SSR-2/1 Req. 52-53 the design distinguishes two heat sinks, which
must not be conflated:

- **Normal heat sink - the Black Sea, once-through seawater**
  (condenser, component cooling and thermal-storage rejection).
  **Non-safety-classified**, graded per IAEA TECDOC-1936 because the
  safety function does not depend on it.

- **Safety ultimate heat sink - the passive internal in-containment
  refuelling-water storage (IRWST) pool plus passive containment
  cooling** (atmosphere-coupled), **independent of seawater**,
  supporting at least 240 h conservative passive grace for the 250 m3
  pool, with a nominal estimate of about 286 h (72 h requirement floor).

The grace-period terminology is used consistently in this FER: **72 h** is
retained as the credited minimum requirement floor and as the battery/UPS
autonomy basis where applicable, while the passive heat-sink inventory
calculation gives **>=240 h conservatively** and **about 286 h nominally** for
the 250 m3 IRWST/passive-containment-cooling water inventory. These values
therefore describe different design bases rather than contradictory results.

Consequently, loss of the seawater intake (storm surge, biofouling,
jellyfish or algal bloom, debris, ice) or loss of the thermal-storage
system is a power-conversion event that does not challenge the safety
ultimate heat sink.

### 8.5.2d Operating limit conditions and how they are determined

The operating limits and conditions are the envelope the plant must stay
within: peaking F_Q <= 2.50 / F_DeltaH <= 1.75 design-specific COLR envelope,
MDNBR >= 1.3, primary pressure <= 14.1 MPa (design; 12.8 MPa operating),
steam-generator secondary pressure 4.5 MPa, hot shutdown margin >= 1 %,
fuel centreline below melt, enrichment <= 5.0 wt%, burnup <= 62 GWd/tHM.
Each derives from a **safety limit** (the physical damage threshold:
departure from nucleate boiling, centreline melt, cladding oxidation,
ASME pressure), through an **analytical limit** established by the
plant-specific analysis, to a **protection setpoint** (§8.7) placed with
margin for instrument uncertainty, drift and response time \[ISA-67.04 /
RG 1.105\]. For departure from nucleate boiling: safety limit MDNBR 1.3
-\> analytical limits 1.33 at the COLR steady envelope and 1.57 at the
bounding AOO corner -\> the overtemperature-DeltaT and low-flow trips
actuate before MDNBR reaches 1.3, with the total-channel-uncertainty
allowance.

### 8.5.3 Defence in depth

The criteria map onto the five IAEA defence-in-depth levels \[SSR-2/1
§2.13\]: **Level 1** prevention via inherently negative feedback
(moderator, Doppler and void coefficients all negative) and
departure-from-nucleate-boiling margin; **Level 2** control via the
reactor protection envelope (§8.7); **Level 3** design-basis-accident
control by passive emergency core cooling, emergency feedwater and
passive residual-heat removal (§8.6); **Level 4** severe-accident
management via the conservative no-operator-action grace of at least 240
h, with a nominal estimate of about 286 h and passive containment
cooling; **Level 5** mitigation via the site-boundary emergency planning
zone (<= 0.5 km, substantiated by reference - §8.5.4).

## **8.6 Reactor Safety Systems Design**

### 8.6.1 Accident-prevention strategy: elimination before mitigation

Aegis-40 removes classes of design-basis events by construction:

1.  **Large-break LOCA - eliminated.** The integral vessel contains the
    core, two helical-coil once-through steam generators and the
    pressurizer within one pressure boundary; no large-bore primary
    piping exists. The limiting LOCA reduces to a small break at
    instrument or injection nozzles.

2.  **Rod ejection - eliminated.** Control-rod drive mechanisms are
    internal (in-vessel), with no head-mounted pressure housing whose
    rupture could provide an ejection path. The bounding reactivity
    insertion becomes uncontrolled rod withdrawal (an AOO), limited to
    1.5x10⁻⁵ Deltak/k/s (x50 margin).

3.  **Boron-dilution - eliminated.** The soluble-boron-free core has no
    dilution pathway; hold-down is by hybrid Gd2O3/Er2O3 burnable
    absorber and control rods.

4.  **Pressurizer surge-line break - eliminated.** The integral
    pressurizer in the vessel head has no external surge line.

### 8.6.2 Engineered safety features (passive)

All credited engineered safety features are passive and
de-energize-to-actuate (§8.7.2):

- **Emergency feedwater** - gravity-driven from an elevated tank;
  isolation valves fail open; no pumps.

- **Passive residual-heat removal** - two 100 % natural-circulation
  trains, vessel -> heat exchanger submerged in the internal IRWST pool.
  The 250 m3 pool sinks decay heat for at least **240 h** before makeup
  when a 15% decay-heat uncertainty is included; the nominal boil-off
  estimate is about **286 h**. The governing decay-heat sizing source is
  7.75 MW at shutdown, with the depletion/source-term value reported
  separately in Section 8.11.

- **Passive containment cooling** - condensate return maintains the
  in-containment pool as the heat sink. This - not the seawater system -
  is the safety ultimate heat sink (§8.5.2a).

- **Passive safety injection** - gravity feed from the internal IRWST
  pool on coincident low pressurizer pressure and level.

- **Containment and internal IRWST pool.** Aegis-40 uses a
  CAREM-25/SMART100-style **low-pressure pressure-suppression
  containment with an internal IRWST/passive water inventory**
  (reinforced concrete, steel-lined, about 15 m diameter, design
  pressure 0.414 MPa). The gravity-driven decay-heat and safety-injection
  loops draw from and reject heat to the in-containment pool, needing no
  pumps and no external emergency-core-cooling penetration. The 250 m3
  pool is the conservative >=240 h passive heat sink, with a nominal
  estimate of about 286 h, and is the seawater-independent safety
  ultimate heat sink. At FER stage, the containment pressure-suppression
  response is closed by a reference-supported screening basis: published
  SMART100 CPRSS/SISTA and CAREM-25 pressure-suppression precedents,
  together with NEA/NRC suppression-pool guidance, support the physical
  feasibility of steam condensation and fission-product scrubbing in the
  internal water inventory. A plant-specific containment pressure and
  temperature transient is retained for the next design phase.

### 8.6.2a Reactor shutdown - two diverse and independent means

IAEA SSR-2/1 Req. 46 requires at least two diverse, independent shutdown
systems, one able alone to hold the core subcritical at its most
reactive state. The soluble-boron-free core holds a large
beginning-of-cycle excess (~13,300 pcm on integral gadolinia), and the
strong negative moderator coefficient - a benefit in operation - works
against shutdown on cooldown. The credited chain:

1.  **Control rods (fast trip) - 16 enriched-boron-10 (90 %) B₄C rod
    assemblies.** Gravity-drop on de-energization via the protection
    system or the diverse actuation system; bank worth ~21,437 pcm gives
    a hot-trip subcritical margin of ~7.6 %. The 16-assembly count
    follows the reference-plant rod geometry, but not its methodology:
    the reference plant relies on soluble boron, whereas Aegis-40 is
    boron-free and closes cold shutdown with the boron injection system
    below. The enrichment lives in the solid rods, so the
    soluble-boron-free claim is intact.

2.  **Emergency Boron Injection System (EBIS) - the diverse, independent
    second shutdown system**, credited for **cold subcriticality and
    main-steam-line-break termination**. Passive borated water (~3000
    ppm; gravity head / nitrogen accumulator, fail-open isolation),
    isolated and dormant in normal operation, armed by the diverse
    actuation system. Standalone cold k_adj 0.930; main-steam-line-break
    (stuck-rod) k_adj 0.845.

For the soluble-boron-free core, the mechanical rods are the fast
hot-trip means but are not credited for final cold shutdown at the most
reactive overcooling condition; **the boron injection system is the
required diverse shutdown system of the boron-free safety architecture,
not a backup.** This is achieved without compromising the
soluble-boron-free concept: the normal coolant stays boron-free (the
boron-dilution accident stays eliminated and the moderator coefficient
stays strongly negative), while the injection-system boron is a dormant
emergency reserve - the standby-liquid-control principle.

### 8.6.6 Reactivity control limits (GDC-28)

The reactivity control systems are designed so that the effects of
postulated reactivity accidents cannot damage the reactor-coolant
pressure boundary or deform the core and its supports in a way that
would impair core cooling \[10 CFR 50 App. A GDC-28; SRP 4.3\]:

- the severe reactivity-insertion accident (rod ejection) is eliminated
  by internal drive mechanisms (§8.6.1);

- the bounding remaining insertion - uncontrolled rod withdrawal - is
  limited to 1.5x10⁻⁵ Deltak/k/s (x50 margin) and terminated by the
  flux-rate and overpower-DeltaT trips before any fuel or pressure limit is
  reached;

- boron-dilution reactivity accidents are eliminated (§8.6.1);

- the main-steam-line-break overcooling insertion (~9,300 pcm) is
  terminated by main-steam isolation and boron injection (§8.6.5b).

Because no credible reactivity insertion is prompt-critical or deposits
enough energy to challenge fuel-pellet integrity, the peak fuel enthalpy
stays far below the pellet-cladding-mechanical-interaction and
pressure-boundary damage thresholds; the pressure boundary is not
breached and core geometry is not deformed, so control-rod insertability
and core coolability are preserved.

## **8.7 Instrumentation and Control System Design**

### 8.7.1 Architecture

The instrumentation and control system is organized in five layers with
strict downward non-interference \[IAEA SSG-39; IEC 61513\]: Layer 1

software path exists from non-safety into Class 1E - the distributed
control system taps sensor circuits through qualified one-way isolation,
and the digital twin is fed through a unidirectional data diode \[IEEE
603; IEEE 384; 10 CFR 73.54\].

### 8.7.1b Design criteria and principles

The design follows \[SSG-39; IEC 61513; IEEE 603\]:

- **Critical parameters** - reactivity (neutron flux, source->power
  range), core heat removal (hot- and cold-leg temperature, primary
  flow, steam-generator level, core-exit temperature), coolant-boundary
  integrity (pressurizer pressure), and containment integrity
  (containment pressure and radiation).

- **Redundancy** - safety channels four-fold (Divisions A-D) with
  **2-of-4 voting**, surviving a single failure plus one channel out for
  maintenance, in both the trip and trip-prevention directions.

- **Diversity** - the Diverse Actuation System is platform-diverse
  against protection-system software common-cause failure; sensor
  diversity backs each variable (e.g. core-exit thermocouples back the
  power-range flux).

- **Physical separation** - Class 1E divisions in separate rooms and
  cable trays with rated barriers \[IEEE 384\]; no 1E / non-1E circuit
  shares a raceway without qualified isolation.

- **Single-failure criterion, fail-safe (de-energize-to-trip), and
  five-layer defence in depth** with upward-only signal flow.

### 8.7.3 Engineered Safety Features Actuation System

The same four-division 2-of-4 structure, with **latching** actuations
(deliberate operator reset). Functions: passive safety injection (low
pressurizer pressure and level), containment isolation (high containment
pressure or radiation), emergency feedwater (low steam-generator level
or loss of normal feedwater), residual-heat-removal /
containment-cooling alignment (low steam-generator level with feedwater
unavailable, or high core-exit temperature post-scram), main-steam
isolation (high steam-line radiation or containment pressure), and
**cogeneration-interface isolation** (fail-closed on high
intermediate-loop or product activity, or on a
steam-generator-tube-rupture signal - §8.8.9, per SSR-2/1 Req. 35). All
actuated devices move to the safe state on loss of motive power.

### 8.7.4 Diverse Actuation System

Class 1E but **platform-diverse** (different technology and
implementation basis from the protection system), covering a postulated
common-cause failure of all four protection divisions \[SECY-93-087; BTP
7-19; SSG-39 §6\]. It monitors power-range flux and pressurizer pressure
through fixed hardware-biased logic with setpoints staggered beyond the
protection envelope, and provides diverse paths to the trip breakers,
safety injection, residual-heat removal, and the boron injection system
(§8.6.2a) on an ATWS signature (high flux/temperature with no confirmed
rod insertion). It is the sole actuation path for the boron injection
system, which carries the ATWS burden in this boron-free core.

### 8.7.5 Control room, control philosophy and human factors

A two-operator main control room per NUREG-0700: reactor-operator and
balance-of-plant consoles, a shift-technical-advisor station, and a
4x80″ overview wall (plant mimic, safety parameter display,
critical-safety-function status). Alarm management per ISA-18.2 (<= 10
alarms in a 10-minute window under design-basis transients);
computer-based procedures with paper backup; hardwired,
software-independent manual actions (reactor trip, safety injection,
containment isolation, emergency feedwater, main-steam isolation) per
IEEE 603 §5.8. A physically and electrically separated **Remote Shutdown
Station** (separate fire area, independent 1E division) provides
post-accident monitoring \[RG 1.97\] and diverse manual trip and
engineered-safety-feature actuation (including boron injection) to reach
and hold safe shutdown if the main control room is uninhabitable
\[SSR-2/1 Req. 65-66\]; its scope is minimal because the plant is
passively safe for at least 240 h without operator action on the
conservative basis.

**Figure 8.7-3.** Main-control-room floor plan (console arrangement,
overview wall, sight lines, circulation). \> \[Figure to be inserted.\]

The ergonomic design is developed through the NUREG-0700 / NUREG-0711
human-factors-engineering process: reconfigurable operator consoles
sized to the 5th-95th-percentile reach and sight envelopes; unobstructed
sight lines from both positions to the overview wall; uniform
control-display conventions and coding; alarm prioritisation and
suppression to keep operator workload low. The conservative passive-safe
grace of at least 240 h means no time-critical operator action is
required - the strongest ergonomic risk-reducer; the nominal pool
boil-off estimate is about 286 h. Main-control-room habitability keeps
operator dose within GDC-19 limits.

**Load-following control philosophy.** Load-following is achieved
primarily through the thermochemical energy store (§8.9), not through
deep reactor power manoeuvring: the reactor is held near baseload while
the store absorbs the dispatch swing. This keeps the reactor in the
regime where its negative feedbacks and power distribution are best
characterized and does not aggravate the peaking, xenon or
moderator-coefficient challenges - a control-side safety benefit. The
distributed control system coordinates turbine throttle, storage
charge/discharge, and the cogeneration-interface isolation interlocks.

### 8.7.6 Digital twin (advisory)

A non-safety digital twin mirrors all Layer 1 signals through the data
diode and provides soft sensors (MDNBR and fuel-centreline estimates),
anomaly detection and predictive maintenance. It is Category C software,
cannot actuate any device, and presents alongside - never in place of -
qualified indications. It enables condition-based maintenance and
reduced-staffing operation without touching the Layer 1-3 safety
qualification \[NUREG-0711\].

### 8.7.7 Qualification

Class 1E equipment is environmentally qualified per IEEE 323 (150 °C,
0.5 MPa, 100 % steam, 1 MGy lifetime, jet impingement where exposed),
seismically qualified per IEEE 344 at SSE 0.3 g, and secured per 10 CFR
73.54 / RG 5.71 (Purdue-model segmentation, no wireless in safety areas,
no remote access to Class 1E, signed firmware, write-once audit logs).

## **8.8 Auxiliary Systems Design**

### Scope and design-driven simplification

Auxiliary and supporting systems sustain normal operation, provide
defence-in-depth (DiD) backup and limit dose (ALARA), without themselves
being required to prevent core damage - the fundamental safety functions
are discharged passively (§8.4, §8.6) \[IAEA SSG-62; SSR-2/1 Rev. 1\].
Three Aegis-40 design choices directly **shrink the auxiliary-system
burden**, and this is treated as a design advantage rather than generic
PWR practice:

- **Soluble-Boron-Free (SBF) core** - eliminates the boric-acid make-up,
  boron-recovery and chemistry-control plant of a conventional CVCS,
  reducing the auxiliary-building footprint, liquid radioactive-waste
  volume and radiological source term.

- **In-vessel control rod drive mechanisms (CRDM)** - no external
  rod-drive housings, seals or dedicated CRDM cooling system to support.

- **Natural-circulation primary loop** - no reactor coolant pumps, hence
  no pump seal-injection, lube-oil, component-cooling or large motor
  electrical loads to serve.

Quantitative performance values quoted in this section are preliminary
reference values, drawn from comparable iPWR practice (NuScale VOYGR,
CAREM-25), and are used as FER-stage reference design values and are
controlled by the final safety and layout bases.

### 8.8.1. Fission-Product Release Control

**Purpose.** Confine radioactive fission products through successive
barriers and, for any release path, filter and monitor effluent so that
public dose stays within limits and the 0.5 km Emergency Planning Zone
design goal is supported (§8.1, §8.5) \[SSR-2/1 Rev. 1; NS-G-1.13\].

**Operating principle.** Three DiD barriers act in series - the fuel
matrix and Zircaloy-4 cladding, the integral RPV / primary pressure
boundary, and the compact containment. Any leakage is captured by the
controlled-area HVAC held at negative pressure, routed through a HEPA ->
charcoal-iodine -> HEPA filter train, and released only via the monitored
stack. On a high-radiation or containment-isolation signal, containment
isolation valves close (fail-safe) and the Standby Gas Treatment System
(SGTS) maintains the secondary-confinement underpressure.

**Layout.** Sources are nested within the below-grade containment;
isolation valves sit at the containment boundary; filter trains and SGTS
are in dedicated HVAC rooms; the release path terminates at the
monitored stack. **Figure 8.8-2** gives the release-control flow/P&ID.

**Safety function.** Limit fission-product release in normal operation
and design-basis accidents; classified per SSG-30. The **SBF core lowers
the gaseous/liquid source term** (no boron-laden effluent), and the
integral pump-free primary loop minimises containment penetrations and
leak paths.

**Performance.** HEPA >=99.97 % at 0.3 µm; charcoal >=99 % elemental
iodine; SGTS 2x100 % trains; negative-pressure cascade maintained on a
single active failure; stack effluent continuously monitored for noble
gas, iodine and particulate activity.

**Maintenance.** Bag-in/bag-out filter change-out, DeltaP trending, in-place
DOP/halide efficiency testing \[RG 1.52\]; periodic isolation-valve
stroke tests and stack-monitor calibration.

### 8.8.2. Ventilation and Air Conditioning (HVAC)

**Purpose.** Control temperature, humidity, cleanliness and the

### 8.8.3. Compressed-Air Systems

**Purpose.** Supply clean, dry, oil-free air to pneumatic instruments

### 8.8.4. Fuel Handling and Storage Systems

**Purpose.** Receive, store, load and unload fuel while maintaining
sub-criticality, cooling, shielding and confinement \[SSR-2/1 Req. 80;
SSG-63\].

**Operating principle.** The complex has three sub-systems - fresh-fuel
storage and handling, spent-fuel storage and handling, and the
transport-and-technological system. Fresh assemblies are received in
transport casks, inventoried and stored dry on criticality-safe
cantilever racks in a dedicated Class-1 fresh-fuel store (air medium),
sized for one full core plus an operational reserve. Refuelling is
carried out under the below-grade cavity with the integral head and
upper internals removed; the refuelling machine transfers assemblies
underwater to the spent-fuel pool (biological shielding and decay-heat
removal), under remote control with height, load and anti-tip
interlocks. Pool cooling and cleanup are by redundant trains with
passive gravity makeup from the IRWST, and fuel-rod cladding
leak-tightness is monitored. After pool residence the assemblies are
loaded into dual-purpose transport/storage casks and moved to an on-site
dry cask store (ISFSI) with passive air cooling.

**Layout.** New-fuel vault -> refuelling cavity -> transfer canal ->
spent-fuel pool -> cask-loading station -> on-site dry cask store (ISFSI).
**Figure 8.8-5** shows the fuel route and storage.

**Safety function.** Maintain **k_eff <= 0.95** in the spent-fuel pool.
With the flux-trap Boral / Metamic B₄C rack, burnup credit and 2000 ppm
pool soluble boron credited, the analysed reactivity is **k_adj = 0.892
<= 0.95** (95/95, including bias and uncertainty); the unborated racked
configuration remains below 1.0, so both sub-limits of 10 CFR 50.68(b)
are satisfied (full criticality basis in §8.11) \[ANSI/ANS-8.1; 10 CFR
50.68b; SSG-27\]. The pool soluble boron does not conflict with the
soluble-boron-free reactor concept - it is credited only for
spent-fuel-pool reactivity, never for reactor reactivity control. The
once-through, single-batch core (37 assemblies; 2224 EFPD about  6.1 EFPY;
discharge burnup 29.6 GWd/tHM; §8.1.5) eliminates periodic refuelling:
fuel handling is limited to the initial core load and the whole-core
discharge at end of cycle, minimising handling frequency and
occupational dose.

**Performance.** Pool bulk <= 50 °C; whole-core decay heat is about
7.8-7.9 MW at shutdown, falling to about 48 kW at 1 year and
single-digit kW at 10 years (§8.11), giving a long passive boil-off
grace period; the pool is sized for one full-core offload (37
assemblies) plus reserve, and the on-site dry store for at least two
discharged cores (about 12 years of operation) \[SSG-15\].

**Maintenance.** Pool level/temperature instrumentation, liner
leak-chase, periodic crane/refuelling-machine load testing.

### 8.8.9. Cogeneration Interface Isolation (Radionuclide Confinement to the Heat / Hydrogen User)

**Purpose.** Ensure that no process transports radionuclides to the
district-heat or hydrogen user in operational or accident states
\[SSR-2/1 Rev. 1 Req. 35\].

**Operating principle.** A **non-radioactive intermediate loop** is
interposed between the reactor secondary steam and both customer
circuits (the thermochemical district-heat network and the solid-oxide
electrolyser), so reactor steam never contacts district-heat water or
electrolyser feed. The intermediate loop is held at **higher pressure
than the reactor-side stream at each interface heat exchanger**, so any
tube leak flows **inward** (clean -> reactor), never outward to the user.
This gives **>= 3 independent barriers** between the primary coolant and
the product: (1) steam-generator tube wall, (2) intermediate-loop
boundary, (3) customer-side heat-exchanger wall.

**Layout.** Interface heat exchangers at the reactor-secondary boundary;
the intermediate loop feeds the thermochemical-storage and electrolyser
heat exchangers; the thermochemical store and electrolyser sit
downstream of the barrier set. **Figure 8.8-16**.

**Safety function.** Confine radioactivity to the nuclear island under
all conditions. Accident-condition isolation is by **fail-closed ESFAS
valves** actuated on high intermediate-loop or product activity, on a
steam-generator-tube-rupture signal, or on containment isolation
(§8.7.3). Tritium is the governing nuclide at the high-temperature (~800
°C) electrolyser interface, addressed by permeation-barrier coatings, a
hydrogen-product tritium monitor and an intermediate-loop getter.

**Performance.** Inward pressure bias maintained at every interface; >= 3
barriers on any radionuclide transport path; fail-closed isolation on

### **8.9.1 Zeolite-13X/water TCES reference and benchmark basis**

The reference thermochemical store is zeolite-13X with water-vapour
adsorption/desorption. Heat is stored by drying the zeolite bed during
charge and released when water re-adsorbs during discharge. Energy is
held as a dry-versus-hydrated material state, so there is no continuous
sensible-heat standby loss. The water working fluid and ammonia-free
inventory are the main reasons this store is selected as the reference
for a nuclear-site industrial island.

The zeolite store is supported by an independent sizing check. A
first-principles calculation based on published adsorption enthalpy,
working water uptake and packed-bed density gives an energy-density
estimate within about 3% of the plant-level balance used for the 200
MWh_th storage block. This provides a zeolite-13X/H2O benchmark in
addition to the separate ammine-store validation. Published
sorption-thermal-storage reviews provide the material-property basis for
the zeolite/water reference store (Yu et al., 2013; Scapino et al.,
2017; N’Tsoukpoe et al., 2009).

## **8.10 Facility Layout Design**

### 8.10.1. Overall Layout Description

The Aegis-40 is a 125 MWth / ~40 MWe (net) integral Pressurized Water
Reactor (iPWR) for coastal deployment at the Sinop site on the Turkish
Black Sea coast, The general arrangement is developed to satisfy four
governing objectives simultaneously: **inherent nuclear safety,
construction efficiency and modularity, economic competitiveness, and
reliable long-term operation and maintenance** over the 60-year design
life.

The defining spatial characteristic is the complete integration of the
primary pressure boundary. The Reactor Pressure Vessel (RPV) houses the
core, two helical-coil once-through steam generators (OTSGs), and the
integral pressurizer within a single pressure boundary. Primary
circulation is driven entirely by natural convection, with no reactor
coolant pumps. This integral, pump-free architecture removes all
large-bore primary piping, eliminates the large-break loss-of-coolant
accident (LB-LOCA) as a design-basis event, and substantially reduces
the Reactor Building footprint.

Following IAEA plant-grouping practice, the site is organised into a
**Nuclear Island (NI)** (Seismic Category I / Class 1E; Protected
security area), a **Turbine (Conventional) Island** (Seismic Category II
power conversion, switchyard and once-through seawater cooling; Vital
security area) and a Cogen (Industrial) Island (Seismic Category III,
non-safety: thermochemical district-heat store, SOE hydrogen unit and H2
storage; NFPA 2; Additional security area, H2 set back >=100 m). The
Turbine and Cogen islands together form the secondary-side Energy
Island. Seismic category is a per-building property, not a property of
the island - e.g. the Control Building sits in the Turbine Island for
MCR-turbine adjacency but is Seismic Cat I. The overall site arrangement
and security-area zoning are shown in **Figure 8.10-1**. Governing
layout constraints are the about 0.5 km Emergency Planning Zone, the 0.3 g
SSE foundation basis, consolidated containment penetrations, and the
NFPA-2 hydrogen explosion stand-off.

### 8.10.2. Site Zoning and the Combined Energy Island

A key layout decision for the Aegis-40 is that the three secondary-side
consumers - the **steam turbine, the Thermochemical Energy Storage (TCES)
district-heating module, and the Solid Oxide Electrolysis (SOE) hydrogen
unit** - are all co-located within a single Energy Island and fed from
**one main-steam corridor** leaving the Reactor Building. Because all
three loads draw from the secondary side (the turbine from the main
steam header, the TCES from a turbine-extraction branch at approximately
1.0 MPa / 180 °C through an intermediate heat exchanger, and the SOE
from the deaerator-inlet steam during off-peak hours), a single
below-grade steam corridor with short branch take-offs serves the entire
island. This minimises high-energy piping length, the number of
Reactor-Building penetrations, and the overall footprint, while
simplifying isolation and in-service inspection. The steam-distribution
arrangement is shown in **Figure 8.10-2**.

The Turbine Generator Building (TGB) is oriented with its condenser end

### 8.10.7. Scalability and Multi-Unit Roadmap

While the reference design is a single 40 MWe unit, the arrangement is
laid out to support the staged multi-unit deployment envisaged in the
PER (5-10 units toward Turkey’s SMR targets). Additional units replicate
the NI module and share common infrastructure (intake/discharge,
switchyard, access, and the Energy Island services), enabling phased
construction and economies of scale on the same site, consistent with
the NuScale multi-module precedent.

## **8.11 Nuclear Waste Management**

Aegis-40 addresses waste on the three fronts mandated by
Technical-Specification Section 4.3.2: (i) an innovative fuel-cycle
design minimising spent-fuel quantity and radioactivity per unit energy;
(ii) a back-end plan with the three required analyses (source term,
decay heat, storage criticality); (iii) minimisation of secondary
radioactive waste. All numbers below are from the final STAT_FINAL
depletion record (ENDF/B-VIII.0, corrected material volumes,
2026-07-03).

### **8.11.1 Waste-minimization by design (fuel cycle)**

The once-through waste basis is 2224 EFPD (6.09 full-power years), 29.6
GWd/tHM core-average discharge burnup and 9.39 tHM initial heavy-metal
loading. The calculated waste intensity is 4.40 tHM/TWhe, based on ~2.14
TWhe lifetime electrical generation. The lower-power-density long-cycle
design eliminates refuelling entirely and supports a simple once-through
baseline.

*Table 8.11-1 Waste minimization*

| Quantity                       | Aegis-40 (once-through) | CAREM-25          |
|--------------------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|
| Thermal / electric power       | 125 MWth / 40 MWe       | 100 MWth / 27 MWe |
| Net efficiency                 | 0.320                   | 0.270             |
| Discharge burnup               | 29.6 GWd/tHM            | 24.0 GWd/tHM      |
| HM discharged per cycle        | 9.39 tHM / ~6.09 FPY    | \-                |
| Assemblies discharged per year | ~6.1 FA/yr              | \-                |
| Waste intensity                | 4.40 tHM/TWhe           | 6.43 tHM/TWhe     |

Even at the deliberately low once-through burnup, Aegis-40 produces ~32
% less heavy metal per unit electricity than CAREM-25, driven by the
higher net efficiency. No assembly exceeds 42 GWd/tHM - a \>30 % margin
to the 62 GWd/MTU fuel-qualification ceiling even in the hottest
position, so cladding-integrity margins are preserved into storage. The
lower burnup (vs a multi-batch scheme) is the accepted price of the
safety- and nonproliferation-led once-through cycle (Section 8.2.3); the
LRM 3-batch equilibrium option (~44.4 GWd/tHM) would cut the intensity
to ~2.9 tHM/TWhe if ever adopted.

### **8.11.2 Back-end fuel-cycle management plan**

Aegis-40 uses a once-through baseline with no reprocessing. At EOC, the
whole core is discharged to the spent-fuel pool, cooled and shielded on
site, then transferred to dry storage or a national spent-fuel
management pathway according to future regulatory requirements.

### **8.11.4 Spent-fuel storage criticality (Section 4.3.2)**

Spent-fuel storage criticality is evaluated separately from the
soluble-boron-free reactor-coolant concept. The credited spent-fuel-pool
storage basis uses fixed flux-trap absorber racks, burnup credit and
approximately 2000 ppm soluble boron in the pool water as a
defence-in-depth measure. This does not contradict Aegis-40’s
soluble-boron-free operating concept because soluble boron is not used
for normal reactor-coolant reactivity control.

Criterion k(95/95) \<= 0.95 follows NUREG-0800 and 10 CFR 50.68. For the
29.6 GWd/tHM once-through discharge composition, the credited rack
configuration with fixed Boral / Metamic B4C absorber, burnup credit and
2000 ppm pool boron gives k_adj = 0.892, including bias and uncertainty
allowance. The unborated racked configuration remains below 1.0,
satisfying the independent subcriticality basis. Fresh or low-burnup
assemblies are administratively restricted to Region-I flux-trap storage
locations.

Table 8.11-3 - Storage-rack criticality basis

| Configuration                               | Credited controls                                                  | k_adj / criterion | Verdict |
|---------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------|---------|
| Burnup-credited spent fuel in absorber rack | Fixed Boral / Metamic B4C rack, burnup credit, 2000 ppm pool boron | 0.892 \<= 0.95    | PASS    |
| Unborated racked sensitivity                | Fixed absorber rack and geometry, no soluble-boron credit          | \< 1.0            | PASS    |

The storage-boron inventory is a spent-fuel-pool criticality control and
is not part of normal reactor reactivity management. Criticality bias
confirmation for a licensing-grade calculation would use the OpenMC and
ENDF/B-VIII.0 bias basis from OECD/NEA burnup-credit benchmarks and
SFCOMPO comparisons. Burnup-credit validation is tied to SFCOMPO and
ICSBEP-type benchmark practice (OECD-NEA, 2013; OECD-NEA, 2020).

### **8.11.5 Secondary radioactive-waste minimization**

The soluble-boron-free design eliminates the borated-water
secondary-waste streams a boron-controlled PWR generates - spent
ion-exchange resins, evaporator concentrates, tritiated boron effluent
from the CVCS - a “reduce-quantity” benefit not captured in the tHM/TWhe
metric. Remaining operational wastes (filters, dry active waste,
clean-up resins) are managed by best-available-technique segregation,
volume reduction and conditioning per IAEA GSR Part 5; wastes are
collected and segregated by nature for their subsequent
processing/destination (not mixed with streams of different
characteristics). The integral-vessel layout with no large primary
penetrations further limits contaminated-component arisings.

**Economic advantages.**

1.  **Cogeneration revenue stacking.** Aegis-40 monetises three products
    where the peers sell mainly electricity. District heat (\$26/MWh-th
    vs \$50 price) and hydrogen (\$2.49/kg vs \$5 price, and vs \$3-6/kg
    green hydrogen) are both produced below market; together they are
    ~12 % of revenue and turn the NOAK case value-positive.

2.  **Load-following via thermal storage** - dispatchable /
    peak-shifting value that baseload-only reactors cannot offer,
    without deep reactor manoeuvring.

    is available where fuel economy is prioritised.

4.  **Compact footprint + small emergency planning zone (<= 0.5 km)** -
    siting and land-cost advantage.

small-plant penalty; the derived value sits above the vendor target, so
competitiveness is explicitly a bet on modular production driving costs
below the scaling curve. The derived case is carried as the upper band
throughout.

**Cogeneration product costing (exergy allocation).** Allocating the
total annualized cost across the three products by exergy (electricity β
= 1.0; district heat β about  0.35 at ~180 °C; hydrogen β = 1.0 by lower
heating value) gives each product its own levelized cost. Electricity
carries 93 % of the exergy stream, so it bears most of the shared cost
and the by-products come out below market: at the NOAK case, LCOH about 
\$26/MWh-th (vs \$50 price) and LCOH2 about  \$2.49/kg (vs \$5 price). These
are floor estimates for the by-products, since a fully attributional
cost would additionally load the conversion-plant capital onto them.

A multi-attribute comparison ranks Aegis-40 second of four (NuScale \>
Aegis-40 \> CAREM-25 \> SMART), stable across weighting schemes, leading
the field on cogeneration, boron-free operation and long-cycle
operation. Reference-reactor comparisons use publicly documented
NuScale, CAREM and SMART information (NRC, 2023a; Villarino et al.,
2000; In et al., 2002).

**Basis.** Generation, fuel throughput and exergy shares are computed
from the design; the fuel-cycle cost uses design physics with cited
market prices (U₃O₈ \$85.75/lb, SWU \$176); capital, operating cost and
the electricity price (\$75/MWh, swept \$60-100, Turkish/European
wholesale) are cited and swept. Levelized costs and net present value
inherit the capital and price uncertainty and are therefore reported as
bands.

## **Appendix B - Containment Heat-Removal Reference Studies**

This appendix records how containment heat removal is treated at FER
stage. The submitted Aegis-40 baseline is the compact low-pressure
pressure-suppression containment with an internal 250 m3 IRWST/passive
water-inventory heat sink described in Sections 8.5-8.10. The FER
contains a plant-specific mass-and-energy screening calculation for the
limiting SBLOCA blowdown into the IRWST and uses published
pressure-suppression/IRWST evidence for phenomena that require a
qualified containment-transient code. The screening basis is not a
licensing substitute; it defines the credible physical mechanisms,
acceptance criteria and the exact follow-on analyses required in
detailed design.

## **Appendix C - Limitations and Next-Step Licensing Analyses**

The FER establishes a coherent conceptual and preliminary detailed-design
basis, but it does not claim licensing-grade closure for every safety and
cost item. The principal next-step analyses are: (i) extension of the
plant-specific SBLOCA suppression-pool mass-and-energy screen into a
qualified time-dependent containment pressure/temperature transient,
including non-condensable gases, pool thermal stratification,
vent/submergence geometry, scrubbing/decontamination factors, leakage and
hydrogen management; (ii) extension of the FER-stage transient analysis
into an integrated system-code DBA package for the limiting AOO, DBA and
DEC-A event set, including consistent primary-system, secondary-system,
containment, protection-logic and passive-safety actuation models; (iii)
fuel-performance confirmation using a qualified fuel-performance code
such as FRAPCON, FRAPTRAN or FAST for temperature, fission-gas release
and cladding limits; (iv) ASME Section III pressure-boundary
finite-element stress categorization, fatigue, fracture-mechanics and
seismic qualification; (v) site-specific radiological consequence and
dose analysis using the final source term, meteorology and
emergency-planning assumptions; (vi) higher-statistics confirmation of
the preliminary operational shielding dose calculation and spent-fuel
cask shielding model; and (vii) independent validation of capital cost,
construction schedule, fuel-cycle cost and cogeneration revenue
assumptions. These items are
identified as controlled detailed-design and licensing work rather than as
unresolved contradictions in the FER baseline.
